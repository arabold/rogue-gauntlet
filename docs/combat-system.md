# Combat actions and active defense

Implemented rules for players and monsters. See [weapon attacks](weapon-attack-system.md) for attack authoring and spells, [monster authoring](monster-authoring-design.md) for AI policy, and the [combat harness](combat-harness.md) for diagnostics. [Actions and interactions](actor-actions-and-interactions.md) covers controls, quick items and timed world interactions.

## Action ownership

Each actor can own one committed action: attack, dodge, guard, item use, interaction or forced reaction. The actor's `ActorActionGateComponent` is wired to every executor's `ActionGate` export, `AttackController` included. Rejected requests change nothing. Dodge or guard can replace attack startup, and dodge can replace attack recovery. A gate with `RecoveryCancels` (the player's) also lets guard replace attack recovery, the next [combo step](weapon-attack-system.md#primary-combos) replace primary recovery, and a cast from the other wand take over as soon as a spell releases. Monsters keep their recovery committed, so it stays the window in which they can be punished. Active contact remains committed. Committed item use prevents voluntary replacement. Stagger interrupts voluntary actions; death cancels and disables them. Ordinary movement remains separate but obeys the current action's restrictions. The current action may report hyper-armor (see [poise](#poise-and-hit-reactions)); any change of ownership clears it. While it reports an [uninterruptible](#poise-and-hit-reactions) window, the gate refuses every other action, stagger included, and only death ends it.

Player presses of primary, secondary, dodge and guard wait `PlayerInputController.InputBufferWindow` for an action that can accept them, so a press made during an attack's contact runs as soon as its recovery begins instead of being dropped. An accepted dodge or guard drops older presses, and a buffered guard press still needs the button held. Holding primary repeats complete attacks without cancelling recovery.

The executor owns its timing and releases ownership on completion or interruption. Movement and animation follow that same commitment; stale callbacks cannot complete an old action or cancel a newer one. Shared authored Resources are definitions: accepted actions snapshot their settings, so live edits cannot change an action halfway through.

`AttackExecution` owns the attack phase and elapsed clock (`WindUp`, `Active`, `Recovery`), including the once-per-execution attacker hit-stop. `AttackController` owns action-gate leases, contact sampling, projectile release and interruption. Effects and debug switches observe the execution; they never drive phase transitions.

Dashes (a monster rush or hop-back) use a `DashComponent` beside `MovementComponent`, like `DodgeComponent`. It acquires the action gate, runs wind-up, travel and recovery through an `AttackExecution`, and drives the mover through a locomotion override. `MoveAndSlide` resolves travel against the world, a blocked dash stops instead of extending, navigation checks a clear corridor first, and pushes, status locks, stagger and death interrupt it. A dash grants no immunity and deals no damage.

## Contact feedback

After the hurtbox resolves a contact, one immutable result feeds target and camera feedback. `CombatFeedbackController` plays a short target flash and material-selected particles on every damaging hit, damage numbers, cosmetic flinch recoil, and a physical attacker lunge on successful melee contact. The lunge moves the attacker's body through `MovementComponent` for a bounded distance, once per execution; walls stop it, and status locks, stagger and knockback take priority. It is a deliberate gameplay movement that closes a small gap after a confirmed hit. The effect and impact-surface profiles are assigned in the actor scene; a contact without an attacker profile, such as a trap's, uses the target hurtbox's `FeedbackProfile`. `CombatCameraFeedbackController` scales its shake from the resolved outcome, softer for a block. Feedback never rolls damage, evaluates defense or pushes anyone: the attack's own knockback is the only defender push, and target recoil is a visual model offset. Sound remains deferred.

Monsters, nests and the practice target show floating damage numbers through a `DamageNumbersComponent` wired to their hurtbox: gold and larger for a critical hit, orange for a rear or sneak hit, grey for blocked chip damage, and a short word for a dodge, parry or block. An invisible monster shows none.

## Dodge, guard and parry

Dodge combines displacement with an authored immunity interval, recovery, charges and a minimum interval between dodges (`DodgeDefinition`). Immunity avoids contacts with the `Dodgeable` trait: strikes and projectiles. Area contacts (a slam's wave, an explosion, a lingering field) never carry it, so they are escaped only by leaving them; hazards and environmental damage, damage over time included, bypass dodge too. Collision may stop displacement without extending immunity. AI must find a supported, clear destination before dodging.

The player's dodge has two charges that refill one at a time, and evading a contact within its `PerfectWindow` refunds the charge once. Body armor shortens it (`DodgeComponent.DistanceScale` from the `DodgeDistance` stat) without shortening its protection. Monster dodges have one charge without a recharge, so only their cooldown applies. `DodgeDefinition.PassThroughLayers` lets a dodge pass through other actors: the player's passes through the enemies layer, so dodging through a monster puts the player behind it. Temporary collision exceptions keep those bodies from shoving the dodger; hurtboxes stay active and walls stay solid. A dodge that ends inside a body keeps moving at half speed, without immunity, for at most `PassThroughExitTime`, until it is clear. Every exit path (finishing, cancellation, interruption, leaving the tree) restores the mask and removes only the dodge's own exceptions. Monster dodges pass through nothing.

The player's dodge heading comes from current movement input, then the last nonzero direction from the previous 0.08 seconds, then facing; a neutral dodge follows facing rather than backstepping. An input-driven dodge starts at once and accepts a new direction during its first 0.04 seconds, keeping its captured facing, so a correction becomes a sidestep. The correction neither restarts nor extends the dodge's movement, immunity, cooldown or action clock. Direct requests (monster, harness and preview dodges) are locked from the start.

Guard commits to a frontal sector and runs through raising, held protection and release recovery, with an optional single-use opening parry window. Shields guard best; a portable light in the off hand gives a weaker makeshift guard. With an empty off hand, guard falls back to the one-handed weapon's guard or the actor's innate guard. Two-handed weapons and paired melee weapons suppress guard entirely. A shield visual alone grants nothing. Changing either hand cancels guard while keeping defensive cooldowns. A guard definition's `MovementSpeedMultiplier` decides whether its actor moves behind it: the player's guards hold still, while shield-bearing monsters keep closing in at reduced speed in their held `Blocking` pose, since no rig has a guarded walk yet ([guard walking](specs/combat-redesign-plan.md#guard-walking)).

Each hit a monster blocks or parries rolls its defense strategy's `RiposteChance`. On a win it answers on the next physics step: the guard drops without its release recovery and a riposte-eligible attack starts, ignoring its cooldown, armored from its first frame until its contact ends. Attacking into a raised guard is punished rather than holding the monster behind it; only a poise break stops the counter, so the hero must dodge, parry or block it. A riposte still rechecks range, sight and a free [attack slot](#attack-slots); when it cannot start, the guard stays up. Behind a raised guard the AI's own attack requests are refused, so they never hold a counter back.

A parry staggers the attacker for the guard's `ParryStaggerDuration`, long enough to answer, and hits on a staggered actor deal its staggered bonus, so a riposte needs no separate system. A parry inside an uninterruptible window stops the hit, but the attack goes on and the attacker is not staggered. An unparried contact with the `BreaksGuard` trait breaks a raised guard: it returns `GuardBroken`, lands `GuardBreakDamageMultiplier` of its damage before armor and staggers the defender for `GuardBreakStaggerDuration`. The parry window still beats a Heavy attack.

Presentation is assigned in the actor scene. Attacks, defenses and stagger share one pose owner and gameplay clock. Actor animation trees play locomotion, knockback and the actor's own states (item use for the player, spawn and sleep for skeletons), never attacks. One push plays one hit reaction, held at its last frame while the push continues; a knockback that overlaps a stagger shows only the stagger, so an actor that can be staggered assigns a stagger clip to its action gate. Missing required clips make the capability unavailable rather than silently selecting unrelated animations.

## Poise and hit reactions

An actor may assign a `PoiseComponent` to its hurtbox. Its shared `PoiseDefinition` sets the meter, its regeneration, break stagger, staggered damage multiplier, hyper-armor scale, break growth and memory, knockback resistance and whether the floating bar shows it. Each unprotected damaging contact removes the attack's `PoiseDamage`, scaled down while the actor's action is armored. Monster meters and weapon poise come from the [balance model](balance-model.md) (`POISE`): a weapon's poise damage follows its kind, blunt and heavier weapons more, and a monster's meter holds a few such hits by archetype, from none for swarmers to many for gatekeepers.

- **Flinch.** While poise remains, the hit does not interrupt and does not push. Hit particles still play.
- **Break.** The contact that empties poise staggers the actor through its action gate and pushes it by the attack's `Knockback`. The meter stays empty during the stagger, so later hits cannot chain breaks. It refills when the stagger ends, larger by `BreakGrowth` for each break within `BreakMemory`, and otherwise after the regeneration delay.
- **Staggered target.** Contacts on a staggered actor, broken or parried, deal `StaggeredDamageMultiplier` damage before armor. A riposte is simply a hit in that window.
- **Without poise.** Actors without poise (rats, practice targets) follow Diablo II's hit recovery: a hit interrupts the current action only if it deals at least `HurtBoxComponent.InterruptThreshold` of max health, and not again within `InterruptImmunity`, so a fast weapon cannot keep a monster from ever attacking. The interrupted actor reels for `HitRecovery` and is pushed only by the attack's own knockback. Smaller hits, hits inside the immunity and hits on an armored action only flinch.
- **Knockback.** Only an attack's own `Knockback` pushes; it is zero unless authored, so ordinary hits hold their target in place. Every push is a distance in metres, the same for a fast or a slow body: it starts fast and slows at `MovementComponent.PushDeceleration`. Heavy blunt weapons, the default melee secondary, area spells and the Warden's swings author pushes. A body whose `MovementComponent` sets `ResistsKnockback` (the gatekeepers and the stone golem) is never pushed.
- **Forced pushback.** An attack with `ForcePushback` also pushes a defender that would only flinch and interrupts its current action, without a stagger or poise break. Hyper-armor still holds, a running stagger is pushed but not shortened, and knockback-resistant poise only flinches. The default melee secondary and a few monster attacks force pushback.
- **Spell pushes.** Push spells (Gust, the novas, Tremor, Ice Wall) instead carry a `ForcedPush` distance on the contact: every undefended, non-lethal hit is pushed outward, even one armor fully absorbs, independently of poise.
- **Hyper-armor.** Authored per attack as a normalized execution interval, it scales incoming poise damage by the definition's armored scale. A break still interrupts.
- **Commitment.** An attack's `Commit` arms it from that share of its wind-up until its contact ends. Monster attacks commit halfway through their wind-up (the model's `POISE["commit"]`): from then on ordinary hits only flinch, only a poise break stops the swing, and the monster no longer drops it to guard or dodge. The hero's attacks never commit.
- **Interrupted attacks.** A monster attack stopped before its contact window opens costs no cooldown, only the action runtime's short retry delay, so a monster that reels attacks again as soon as it recovers. A parried or completed attack keeps its cooldown.
- **Uninterruptible attacks.** An attack with `Interruptible` off makes its hyper-armor window unstoppable, the committed attack of a boss or elite: only death ends it. Hits there drain no poise and pushes do not cancel it; a parry stops the hit without cancelling the attack or staggering the attacker; the gate refuses every other action, so a monster never guards or dodges out of it; and a freeze waits for the window to close. Its recovery outside the window stays open to punishment.

Attacks author `PoiseDamage`, `Knockback`, `ForcePushback`, `Commit` and `HitStopDuration`. Weapons without a custom definition take their own `PoiseDamage` and `Knockback`, derived from their kind; the default secondary multiplies the weapon's poise, carries hyper-armor and, in melee, breaks guard and forces pushback (`PlayerAttackController`'s default impact exports). Absorbed, blocked, dodged and parried contacts deal no poise damage.

Hit-stop pauses only the attacker's execution clock and pose, once per swing, after a damaging or blocked contact. Poise is runtime-only: loading, floor entry and harness resets start full. Sources without authored poise (traps, scripted hits) deal poise equal to their damage. Status ticks deal no poise damage or hit reaction; burning ticks pass through the hurtbox so elemental wards can absorb them.

## Threat tiers and telegraphs

An attack's `DefenseTraits` (Dodgeable, Guardable, Parryable, BreaksGuard) are the single source of what defenses do against it. Its `AttackThreatTier` is a label derived from them:

| Tier | Derived from | Guard | Parry | Dodge | Indicator tint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | Guardable or Parryable, no BreaksGuard | Blocks with chip damage | If Parryable | If Dodgeable | Normal |
| Heavy | BreaksGuard | Breaks (see above) | If Parryable | If Dodgeable | Orange |
| Unblockable | Neither Guardable nor Parryable | No | No | If Dodgeable | Red |

Each tier sets the shortest wind-up a monster attack may have so a player can read and answer it (`AttackThreatRules.MinimumWindUp`); monster validation enforces it.

Monsters warn with one reusable `AttackIndicatorComponent` (`attack_indicator.tscn`), Normal melee attacks included. It draws the authored melee footprint or projectile lane on the ground with the shared floor overlay (`ground_overlay.gdshader`), so a warning lies over every tile, rug and stair while walls and bodies in front still hide it. It follows aim during wind-up, sharpens at commitment and fades after release; threat tier changes only the tint. The indicator does not choose targets, change timing or affect defense. Monster validation requires it for weapon attacks; the player has none.

The Warden's slash is Normal, so blocking it leaves time to counter during its recovery, while its overhead is Heavy and must be dodged or parried. The player's default melee secondary is Heavy against a monster's guard.

## Projectile warnings

`AttackDefinition.ProjectileAimLockDuration` opts single and spread shots into an aim lock: the shot leaves from the origin and direction captured that long before release, even if the target moves afterward. Zero keeps release-time aiming. During aiming the indicator shows a translucent trapezoid that narrows toward the lane; at commitment it becomes a bar reaching past the target to the attack's range. Release, interruption and target death remove the warning, and an interrupted preparation can never fire later. Walls and invisible shooters suppress it. The executor owns tracking and commitment independently of effects and debug toggles; `AttackController.Indicator` references the actor's indicator. Radial and area-drop patterns need different warning shapes and do not accept the setting.

## Trap hazards

Every trap derives from `Trap`, which puts it in the `trap` group: its hits carry the trap faction, which monsters ignore, though a monster's body still stops a trap's shot. Damage grows with depth through `TrapDamage`, along a line the [balance model](balance-model.md) derives so a trap keeps its share of the hero's health. Traps are tool scripts that act only in a game, because room emission configures them in editor previews too. [Level design](level-design.md#trap-gauntlets) owns where floors place them, and the [room harness](../scenes/debug/README.md#room-generation) plays any gauntlet room.

**Plate traps** are hidden. A concealed `PressurePlate` is a trigger source: its `TriggerOutputComponent` carries each on/off change to its receivers (`ITriggerable`) through a shared `TriggerMode`, Pulse (once per step), Hold (while pressed) or Latch (for good). Plates are found like secret doors or by stepping on them, and finding one warns but never disarms it. A dodge carries the hero over a plate without pressing it (`IFloorContact`); a dodge that touches down on it, as an overloaded hero's short one may, counts as stepping on it. The spike trap pulses a `SpikeBurst` that springs from holes drawn on whatever floor lies there. The other plate traps (`TriggeredTrapProfile`) pair a bare plate, whose slab sinks under the hero (`PlatePressComponent`), with a `WallLauncher` on the wall across from it: a click and, too soon to react, a fast dart or fireball, repeated while the plate is held on. A launcher draws no lane because its warning is the plate, which an attentive hero finds and walks around; standing on it, only moving out of the lane on the click escapes the shot.

**Gauntlets** are visible and rhythmic. A `TrapGauntlet` holds one arrangement of `TrapHazard` children on a shared clock that starts when it enters the tree and pauses with the game, so every load replays the same beat. Each hazard has a period and phase (`HazardBeat`). `TrapGauntlet.PhaseFor` staggers rows by fractions of the beat, never by a walking speed, since heroes walk at different speeds and pause between rows: `Unison`, `Alternate` (every other row half a beat later) or `Wave` (each row a quarter beat after the last). A gauntlet wakes while the hero is within its `ActivationRadius`, beyond the camera's view, of its hazards' danger and that space is explored; asleep, hazards hold still and never warn, fire or cut.

- A `WallSpout` fires one pooled projectile every period through its `TrapShotComponent` (projectile, muzzle, speed, range, element and defense traits, shared with wall launchers): darts from a slit or fireballs from a gargoyle. It warns for at least an Unblockable attack's minimum wind-up with its wind-up effect and the shared indicator's lane, and fires only shots whose whole warning played while it was awake. Shots are dodgeable and guardable; fire ignores armor.
- A `PendulumBlade` swings a blade across the path from a pivot high above the walls, its position a pure function of the clock. Awake, it cuts the hero at most once per swing as a hazard contact that only a dodge avoids, and pushes them out of its path to the side they stood on, to `Clearance` from it whatever their speed: clear of the next swing, short of the next row. Every cut pushes, even one that only flinches.

## Contact resolution

Confirmed contact resolves faction and death filtering, eligible active defense, debug immunity, hit zone and sneak bonus, the staggered multiplier, armor, any active magic ward, health and then the hit reaction. Wards reflect a fraction of absorbed damage after the original contact resolves; reflected contacts cannot reflect again. There is **no passive evasion or accuracy contest**: Dexterity contributes critical chance, not a defense roll. Movement can avoid contact, timed dodge or parry can reject eligible contact, and guard reduces damage before armor. Armor blocks a random amount between zero and its value from each physical hit, but a hit always deals at least the hurtbox's `MinimumDamage` (`ArmorRules`). Elemental damage (missiles, spells, burning, fire traps) passes armor; elemental wards and resistances defend against it instead.

Player and enemy facades forward complete `ICombatDamageable` contacts to their authored hurtboxes. Body or child-collider contact keeps attack identity, delivery kind and defense eligibility, like a direct hurtbox contact. Non-damageable detection areas (interaction, pickup or stairs) are ignored by projectile contact and splash queries.

Dodged and parried contacts suppress damage and hit reactions. Blocks report final chip damage without a hit reaction; guard breaks report their reduced damage and stagger instead. Positive unprotected damage then [flinches, interrupts or staggers](#poise-and-hit-reactions) the defender. Parry interrupts only the execution it actually intercepted, and never an uninterruptible one. Each swing resolves once per defender, a defended contact included; projectile splash cannot hit its directly resolved target again.

Defense eligibility is authored per attack: ordinary melee allows dodge, guard and parry; ordinary projectiles allow dodge and guard; area impacts have independent flags and are never dodgeable. Environmental damage does not automatically grant defense eligibility. Unavoidable content needs clear presentation.

Attacks may carry on-hit status payloads. The hurtbox rolls each only after a direct contact resolves as damaging and leaves the target alive, using the attack or projectile random stream; a full chance consumes no draw. Defended, filtered, absorbed, splash and lethal contacts apply nothing. See [status conditions](stats-and-itemization.md#buffs-and-status-conditions).

## Facing and sneak attacks

A hurtbox may assign a `HitVulnerabilityProfile` (monsters use `humanoid_vulnerability.tres`; the player has none). Direct melee and projectile contacts are classified by where they come from relative to the defender's facing: front, rear or side, within the profile's half-angles. Area, hazard and environmental damage have no zone.

- **Rear hits** deal the profile's rear damage and poise multipliers.
- **Sneak attacks** land on an unaware monster (no target yet, and sleeping, idle, guarding or patrolling). They deal `UnawareDamageMultiplier` damage and, by default, empty its poise. Rear and sneak bonuses do not stack; the larger applies.
- **Noticing.** Awareness is read before the contact; afterwards the monster targets whoever struck it and gives chase.
- **Feedback.** Rear and sneak hits scale the attacker's hit-stop by `EmphasisHitStopScale`, returned in `DamageResolution`; the combat trace records zone and awareness.

Modifiers are deterministic multipliers, never rolls, and apply only to unprotected contacts.

## Attack slots

The player owns an `AttackSlotsComponent` that limits how many monsters may start an attack on the player at once, separately for melee and ranged. A monster reserves a slot when its attack is accepted and returns it when the attack completes or is interrupted; a rejected start returns it at once, and death, target loss or leaving the tree forget it. After attacking, a monster yields for `ReacquireDelay` only while another monster waits for the same kind of slot, so a lone monster keeps its cadence. Ripostes also need a slot. An attack policy can opt out with `UsesAttackSlot`; the gatekeepers do, so minions never stall a boss.

Monsters using `EngagementRingStrategy` approach while a slot is free and otherwise wait on a ring around the player at `RingDistance`, facing it. Ring points (`AttackSlotsComponent.GetRingBearing`) spread by arrival order over an `Arc` centered behind the player's facing, so crowds surround and flank. A monster moving to its point swings at most `OrbitStep` around the player per point, circling instead of walking through it; points snap to the nearest spot its body fits.

Any monster can flank: a combat profile's optional `Flank` (`FlankStrategy`) reshapes what its movement strategy decides. While that strategy would close in from farther than `CommitDistance` and the monster stands more than `Tolerance` off its ring bearing on the flank's `Arc`, it circles to that bearing at `Distance` without attacking. Once in position, closer than `CommitDistance` or after `MaximumDuration` of flanking (against a player who keeps turning to face it), it commits and closes in until its movement strategy stops closing in. Each monster's episodes live in its own `FlankRuntime`. Shared presets live in `scenes/enemies/strategies/`: rats pair `bite_and_dart` with `flank_rear`, so a pack works round to the player's back before it bites; minions pair `engagement_ring` with `surround`, so a line of them splits up and closes in from different sides.

## Enemy decisions and persistence

Enemies react to visible, plausible threats after an authored delay. They cannot read player inputs or reroll each frame: one threat produces one categorical dodge, guard or no-response choice. When the observed attack is unsafe to guard (not Guardable, or BreaksGuard) the policy dodges instead if the monster can, and otherwise does not respond; forced harness modes bypass the policy. Directional checks, cooldowns, pressure and recovery still govern execution. See [monster policies](monster-authoring-design.md#independent-capabilities-and-policies).

Heavy actors can be outmaneuvered:

- **Turn cap.** `MovementComponent.MaxTurnRate` caps all smooth turning. Only explicit facing snaps ignore it, and monster defenses do not use them.
- **Wind-up tracking.** Monster melee wind-ups track their target no faster than the attack's `WindUpTurnRate`, then hold the facing committed `FacingLockLead` seconds before contact (see [reliable melee contact](weapon-attack-system.md#reliable-melee-contact)). Moving sideways in that window can make a committed swing miss.
- **Start angle.** An action's `MaximumStartAngle` only lets it start when the target is within that angle of the monster's facing; a monster in reach but not facing its target holds and turns instead of swinging.
- **Defenses.** A monster raises its guard along its current facing and does not guard against an attacker outside the guard's sector. A riposte turns at the normal rate and is rejected if the target is not yet in front.

The Warden combines all four: a player who gets behind it and stays there draws no swings, but running full circles laps back through its front.

Saving keeps defensive cooldowns, dodge charges with their recharge, and remaining combat commitment. Loading cancels transient actions and restores a neutral lockout without immunity; held guard needs a fresh press after load, pause or focus loss. Stair travel rejects committed actions. [Randomness and saves](randomness-and-seeds.md) owns reconstruction and compatibility rules. Future Dexterity-based defense tuning must affect an explicitly activated action, never introduce passive hit rejection.
