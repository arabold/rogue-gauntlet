# Level design

Implemented generation, room authoring, encounters and visibility. [Dungeon layout, access and discovery](dungeon-exploration-design.md) owns intended realm pools, recipes, locks and secrets; [door access](door-access.md) describes implemented locks/secrets and the [progression plan](specs/level-progression-prototype-plan.md) retains future realm recipes and seals.

See the [folder guide](../scenes/levels/README.md) for code and preset locations, and the [room generation harness](../scenes/debug/README.md#room-generation) for single-room and full-floor previews.

## Current generation

Gameplay uses the [Garrison factory preset](../scenes/levels/garrison/factories/garrison_mixed_room_factory.tres): ordinary and special slots select generated hall, storage, mess hall, barracks, library, sleeping-library, narrow-passage, fenced-walkway, partitioned-storage and prison recipes with a stone-and-wood palette. Barracks and storage compose shared footprint/content policies and optional columns. Barracks offer opposing bed rows, perimeter sleeping bays and L-shaped layouts; storage offers rectangular and L-shaped bays. Their former predefined room scenes are removed. Entrances and exits remain authored. Caves, sewers and other templates remain available in the general development factory.

This is a curated first-realm preset, currently shared by all depths, rather than a realm/depth resolver. Per-depth room roles and required routes remain proposed. In the builder Inspector, **RandomPurposeMask** limits random purposes; a fixed **Purpose** overrides it. Empty/unknown random masks fail before replacing existing geometry. The generic room builder remains available for other presets. The editor’s **Coded Generator Preset** selects a room recipe; the arrangement playground uses the gameplay factory.

Generation packs and rotates rooms around an entrance, prefers a geometrically distant exit, connects rooms and explicit doorways, resolves walls/doors, reserves optional secret pockets, furnishes procedural rooms, decides special encounters, assigns validated lock/key pairs, then places other loot and bakes navigation. Ordinary encounters use the completed geometry. Ordinary connectivity and a distant exit do not guarantee a mandatory route or encounter order.

Generation hands gameplay a `GeneratedFloor` (`Level.Floor`): the layout, rooms, doors, arrival, descent and encounter plan, plus every spatial query gameplay asks (room lookup, footprint clearance, free spawn and landing positions). The generator runs its own placement checks against the same instance, and freezes it at handoff. Components read seed, depth, floor, run and exploration from their `Level`, never from the generator or the session.

Generation settings and factories are assigned in [level.tscn](../scenes/levels/level.tscn). [Randomness and seeds](randomness-and-seeds.md) owns reproducibility and saved differences. Normal map dimensions are unchanged by the arrangement playground.

## First-realm encounter prototype

The first three depths use the [Sunken Garrison encounter resource](../scenes/levels/encounters/sunken_garrison.tres). Weighted, depth-bounded entries spend authored threat budgets; clearance may reduce the number actually placed. The factory's encounter plan substitutes nests and the gatekeeper for ordinary slots, so budgets alone do not measure final floor difficulty. [Monster definitions](monster-catalog/README.md) supply fixed strength and rewards. Other depths use an explicit development fallback, not finished realm content.

- Depth 1 introduces ordinary skeleton encounters.
- Depth 2 adds rats and finite nests. Nests activate in revealed nearby space, limit simultaneous summons and total slots, and never refill killed slots. Destroying a nest collapses bound survivors without XP or loot.
- Depth 3 adds the Forsaken Warden. Defeat unlocks downward travel through an objective check. Visible seals, boss phases and guaranteed reward-room topology are not implemented.

A room may provide a `GatekeeperSpawnMarker` to control boss position and facing after rotation. Author one intended anchor and ensure its physical clearance. Otherwise generation picks the roomiest supported location nearest the descent, and retries a bounded number of deterministic layouts; failure returns safely instead of loading an invalid floor. The site is decided once, before locks and loot: locks never seal its room, loot keeps clear of its minimum footprint, and the gatekeeper takes the first encounter slot there. Large-actor movement retains its clearance constraints, and tall enemies stay hidden until their tile is discovered.

Cleared enemies, destroyed nests, killed summon slots and Warden defeat persist across revisits. Living actors reset to their generated initial state; partial health, position and summon timers are not saved. See [monster authoring](monster-authoring-design.md) for tactics and the [roadmap](development-roadmap.md#first-three-floor-acceptance) for playtest acceptance.

## Room template creation

Author a self-contained `Room` scene with explicitly assigned floor, wall and decoration GridMaps. Use the shared mesh libraries and include relevant prop collision. Complete authored rooms bring their own content; generic gameplay furnishing/loot does not decorate their interior automatically.

GridMap cells are one world unit with centering disabled. Logical map tiles are **4×4 units centered on their anchor**, even when floor meshes are smaller or larger. Keep tiles aligned to those centers; the room's bounds and rotation derive from geometry, not a manually edited map copy.

Add `DoorwayMarker` nodes for intentional connection points, facing outward. With explicit markers, other edges are sealed; without them, open edges are optional connectors, suitable for caves. A real `Door` makes an opening gated; an opening without one is an archway. Unconnected doors are removed when walls are resolved. Do not infer an opening from a decorative door mesh alone.

The generator rotates complete rooms in quarter-turns, including GridMaps, props and markers. Verify the scene after rotation, especially doorway facing, collider alignment and boss anchors. Keep spaces large enough for the actors and encounters intended to use them.

## Procedural rooms

`ProceduralRoomBuilder` is an Inspector-authored Resource selecting footprint shape, surface materials, architectural purpose, wear, columns, openings and optional chasms. These produce structural variety; reusable furnishings supply small deliberate compositions. Runtime gameplay builds shells before corridor resolution. Standalone editor generation can add arrangements and save an editable room scene.

The room retains its resolved purpose for arrangement eligibility. A saved authored room is a deliberate content choice; ordinary room variety should not depend on stockpiling frozen copies of generated shells. Use the [arrangement authoring guide](../scenes/levels/arrangements/README.md) for the existing generator controls and preview workflow.

### Coded room generators

`RoomGenerator` is a Resource base for coded recipes. Each `.tres` instance assigns an explicit `ShellBuilder` and its content policies/settings. `MixedRoomFactory.RoomGenerators` selects recipes by `Weight`; an empty list uses the existing generic `RoomBuilder`. Entrance/exit anchors retain their existing factory path. Garrison has no authored ordinary/special-room selection.

`ModularRoomGenerator` supplies the shared orchestration for barracks, storage, narrow passages and an alchemist’s sleeping library. Weighted `RoomLayoutVariant` Resources pair a `RoomBaseLayout` (shape, minimum/maximum dimensions and entrances) with a reusable `RoomContentLayout`. Recipes also set a minimum usable floor-cell count. One- and two-tile-wide footprints are supported; content decides whether it fits. L footprints have explicit arm widths and substantial corner recesses. `WallBedsRoomLayout` fills measured wall spans; `PerimeterRoomLayout` places supply bays along the actual boundary, including recessed edges. The shell builder constructs each candidate once; a bounded retry changes an unfit footprint without changing the selected content stream. Generation fails explicitly if a selected recipe cannot fit, instead of substituting an authored room.

`RoomColumnLayout` independently selects column meshes and aisle/perimeter pair patterns. Required furniture and connected passage are checked first; an optional group is accepted only if it preserves both. A null column policy disables that pass. `ColumnChance` controls whether it is attempted; space constraints can still omit groups. Its isolated stream preserves the floor, entrances and required furniture when columns are toggled. Longitudinal and outward adjustments allow full-height pillars beside beds. Short posts are reserved for future fenced spaces. Modular rooms retain the perimeter walls used for validation; the floor generator adds surrounding corridor walls without substituting a different wall profile beside required furniture. Shared Resources remain immutable, while each generated room owns its slots and geometry. Base/content/column presets live under `garrison/layouts/`.

A shell’s weighted `SurfaceStyles` palette chooses one `RoomSurfaceStyle` per candidate: a floor material and an explicit straight/half/corner wall family. Stone, plain light wood and plain dark wood are ordinary choices; cellar palettes also offer earth. Patterned wood is explicit and rarer in formal halls/libraries. Timber-braced walls accompany wood/earth styles; masonry stays a masonry family. Wear and small corner overlays can add local accents. Coded rooms retain these authored wall choices through floor placement.

All coded recipes validate a temporary room before replacing editor geometry. Required slots have stable IDs and exact transforms; compatible slots can share a coarse tile reservation. Required and optional placement share wall support, overlap and passage checks. Exact slots also require support under their entire physical envelope. Sharing a coarse connector tile is allowed when actual geometry keeps its entrance clear; generic optional scatter retains its density policy. Modular furnishing budgets follow the actual required slots. `RoomFurnishingContext` holds per-room reservations shared by furnishing functions. `RoomFurnishingPatterns` composes individual beds, boxes, bedside tables and optional wall decorations, repeats them along measured wall spans, and places table/bookshelf rows. Bed clusters independently vary bedding, optional companions, their side and wall decoration; rejected optional pieces do not remove the bed. Arrangement definitions may assign leaf variations with distinct identities/geometry. Shared helpers and optional pools prefer unused safe choices before repeating, then balance reuse through isolated seeded choices. Missing alternatives remain repeatable. `RoomAssemblyDefinition` and its local `RoomAssemblyPart` entries generalize primary/companion composition for barrel/keg supply stations and workspaces. Companions have independent chance, offset and facing; whole trials preserve circulation. Wall ends/corners are tried first, and perimeter scatter favors corners when the actual geometry fits. `CompositeRoomLayout` runs content functions in order against the same reservations: the alchemist’s room combines a sleeping quarter with library shelves. An empty composition deliberately leaves a passage unfurnished. Dining and library generators also call the shared row helpers; shelf columns/rows determine library dimensions. `RoomBoundaryPalette` and `RoomBoundaryPatterns` assemble individual fencing/half-wall pieces. `RoomBoundaryLayout` creates wall- or corner-attached bays with one opening, left/right fenced walkways with end access, and short dividers. These remain furnishings under the existing exploration rule: furniture does not block fog revelation. Barred chamber rows additionally support operable cell gates; opaque partitions and internal mechanical locks remain [planned](specs/reusable-room-sections-plan.md).

Generators choose required entrances together with their structural layout, before shared floor and wall construction. Generation builds structure first, places/rotates rooms and resolves corridors, then applies each generated room's interior pool using its actual connected entrances. The dungeon-wide arrangement pool is used only for generic procedural rooms. `HallRoomGenerator` owns its dimensions and mirrored column placement: `WidthTiles` is odd, `LengthTiles` lists compatible odd lengths, and the aisle always has an opening at both ends. `SideOpeningChance` controls extra midpoint openings on the side walls; the shell’s generic opening-count settings do not control halls. Its primary decoration pool accepts wall fixtures; `ColumnItems` restricts the allowed column meshes and selects one consistent style per hall. The generic shell builder also honors its configured column palette for every purpose. Modular storage uses a `PerimeterRoomLayout` palette with a required minimum and an upper count. It reuses the perimeter arrangement strategy and promotes accepted placements into required slots; optional dressing remains separate.

The furniture-led concepts additionally place required `RoomFurnishingSlot` child anchors while building structure. Their transforms rotate with the room and resolve into master-map coordinates after placement. `SlotArrangementStrategy` uses those exact anchors and facing (including small offsets for wall trim) through the common collision and passage checks. Every required slot must fit; a failure reports an invalid composition instead of dropping identity-defining furniture. Generic rerolls remove obsolete slots, while saved authored scenes retain their editable furniture and anchors.

| Generator | Composition and sizing rule |
| --- | --- |
| `DiningRoomGenerator` | One to four banquet rows; room length grows by two tiles per row, with end entrances and circulation around the tables. Assign a centered `TableArrangement` with a three-tile footprint and eligible `TableRows`. |
| Modular rooms | Assign compatible `RoomLayoutVariant` presets plus optional columns. Wall beds, supply bays, shelves and ordered composite layouts furnish independently from shape. |
| `PrisonRoomGenerator` | Select one wall or opposing walls and a cell count per wall. Chamber width/depth, minimum aisle and end bays determine the shell. The Garrison preset uses one/two cells per row, stone floors, varied bedding, optional guard tables/chairs and barrel stations. |
| `LibraryRoomGenerator` | `ColumnsPerBank` determines width around a central aisle; a selected `StackRows` count and `StackRowSpacing` determine length. Sections join end to end within a row, while every pair of book-facing rows retains at least `MinimumAisleWidth` clearance. `CornerBookcase` short sections extend perimeter rows toward the wall corners. All pieces remain individually editable. The Garrison preset uses one column per bank and three/five rows to fit the existing gameplay map. |

Each recipe may assign a `RoomDressingProfile`. Required furniture is placed first, followed by intentional wall lighting and a small optional clutter pass. Clutter palettes suit the room: provisions in dining rooms and halls, footlockers in barracks, archive boxes in libraries; cellars use their larger main storage palette. All passes share wall support, collision and connected-passage checks. Selection uses separate derived RNG streams, so optional dressing does not change the structural layout or required furniture.

`RoomLightingProfile` chooses fixtures beside actual connected doorways, optionally in pairs. Barracks may also place lights at assigned bed heads; halls may line their side walls at a configured spacing. Door, pair and bed chances, mounting height, spacing, count, and the lit/unlit ratio are authored per recipe. Fixtures require explicit lit and unlit definitions; the unlit scene disables both its light and fire particles. Unsafe positions are omitted. Libraries mount fixtures above their wall shelves. The room's explicit `LayoutAxis` rotates with the room so hall-side lighting follows its architectural axis.

The in-game **Debug → Dark level** checkbox sets the current `Level.IsDark` flag. Darkness removes ambient light from the explicitly assigned `LightingEnvironment` and extinguishes mounted torch lights and flames through their authored `EnvironmentalLightComponent`. Turning it off restores the original lit/unlit fixture states and ambient intensity. Fog-of-war reveal remains unchanged. The player switches between original broad lighting on normal floors and a dim, short-range baseline on dark floors. This is a temporary override for the current level instance; it is not saved or automatically selected during generation. Seeded dark-floor selection remains [planned gameplay](game-design.md#dark-floors-and-portable-lighting-planned).

The player's unaided lights use short ranges and distance falloff on dark floors; normal floors retain the original player brightness and reach. **Debug → Spawn item → Lamps and torches** offers six usable portable lights. Equip one in either hand through its inventory menu to attach its model, flame and light. The held scene adds illumination only on dark floors, so carrying a light does not brighten normal floors further; inventory and dropped models stay unlit. See [portable-light equipment](stats-and-itemization.md#portable-light-equipment) for hand conflicts and persistence. Fuel and manual ignition remain deferred.

Dimensions are part of a room algorithm’s contract, rather than arbitrary inputs to repair afterward. A generator can require odd widths for central doors, module-sized increments, or bounds derived from its furniture. The hall is the first implementation; the mess hall also derives its dimensions from table count. Entrance-to-entrance carpet routing remains a future extension.

To add a room type, subclass `RoomGenerator`, supply its `CreateInteriorPool()` policy and validation, and override `PopulateStructure()` when custom architecture is needed. Reuse `ProceduralRoomBuilder` for ordinary shells and subclass `ConnectedArrangementStrategy` to filter/weight candidate positions through `CandidateWeight()` while retaining safety checks. More complex coordinated layouts can implement `RoomArrangementPlacementStrategy.Plan()` directly, but must enforce the same passage and collision invariants. For reusable cell/storage/sleeping chambers, `RoomChamberDefinition` supplies dimensions, barred front/partition meshes and an optional door scene. `RoomChamberPatterns` shares dividers between adjacent chambers and emits separate editable pieces into `Room.InteriorStructure`; furnishings use those individual collision bounds. `PrisonRoomGenerator` reuses bed and primary/companion assembly functions. Every cell has an operable gate, and the open-door passage proof connects all cells, the aisle and both room entrances. Gate swing/approach reservations exclude furniture without treating an openable gate as a permanent wall. Optional guard content tries the end bays and the free opposite wall in one-sided prisons, and is omitted where unsafe. Bars follow the existing non-occluding exploration rule. [Interior door access](door-access.md#interior-cell-gates) describes identity, interaction and persistence.

Shared recipe/asset Resources remain definitions; runtime pools duplicate storage definitions before changing repeat limits. Per-room policies travel with placed room regions, use the existing arrangement RNG stream keyed by generation attempt and room ID, and also supply the clearance used for later chest placement. `EnableFurnishings` toggles furniture without changing shell/layout randomness; cell structure and gates remain. Standalone editor generation protects every opening. Gameplay must connect every explicit doorway, including both hall ends; an unrouteable required doorway fails generation before wall placement rather than being sealed. Generic inferred open edges remain optional. Saved generated scenes become authored rooms and are not furnished again. Generation changes invalidate earlier development saves; `SaveGame.CurrentVersion` identifies the supported format.

### Light flicker

Every flickering light shares one Inspector-tunable `LightFlicker` resource that turns noise into a brightness multiplier. Presets live in `scenes/effects/light_flicker/`: `torch` for wall torches, candles and the player's light, `flame` for fire spells and `electric` for lightning strobes. Each light starts its flicker at its own random offset, so the torches spawned with a floor never flicker in step. Standalone lights are `FlickerLight` nodes, which flicker around their `Energy` only while visible; lights whose energy a spell effect already drives apply the flicker through `EffectLight`.

## Doors and enemy navigation

Closed exterior doors block physics, exploration sight and navigation. Barred interior cell gates block traversal while allowing exploration into the cell. Open doors enable their navigation connection; enemies commit to crossing rather than repeatedly steering between sides. Reachability and actual movement use the same door-aware navigation state. [Monster authoring](monster-authoring-design.md#awareness-and-pursuit) owns detection and pursuit rules.

## Map occlusion and fog of war

Permanent black caps hide void under the rotating isometric camera. Gameplay exploration uses bounded omnidirectional line of sight from the player, independent of room-entry events, facing, lighting and camera zoom. Walls, unsanctioned room/corridor edges and closed door thresholds block sight; open doorways reveal only their visible wedge. Exact diagonal corner crossings require both routes to be open. Furnishings do not block discovery in this first implementation. Editor previews expose generated space.

Discovery records an eight-by-eight sample grid per tile. Newly seen samples stay explored permanently, including across travel and save/load; unseen parts of a partially explored tile stay covered. A depth-based screen shader maps rendered surfaces back to this world-aligned history, feathers the boundary inward and fades newly discovered space into view. Feathering does not change authoritative discovery. There is no dimmed remembered-space state or current-line-of-sight requirement for already discovered content.

Exploration belongs to the level's `FloorDiscovery` node ([floor_discovery.tscn](../scenes/levels/floor_discovery.tscn), wired to `Level.Discovery`). `SightRadius` controls reach in world units (default 24), `FadeSeconds` how long newly seen space fades in (default 0.35 seconds) and `FeatherWidth` how far the fog feathers inward (default 1 world unit). Each floor owns its exploration texture and publishes it with the map bounds and fade clock as `fog_*` global shader uniforms ([fog_of_war.gdshaderinc](../scenes/levels/fog_of_war.gdshaderinc)), so the fog and the silhouettes above it share one coverage test. Sight updates at most ten times per second while moving, and refreshes after door changes; a closed door seals its threshold because sight reads the door itself. The fade clock stops once a reveal has faded in. Exploration is independent of normal/dark-floor illumination.

Content gates retain tile-based discovery: discovering any sample on a tile enables its loot, effects and eligible x-ray silhouettes. The existing `ExplorationGateComponent` hides torch visuals, particles and lights until that tile is discovered. Previously discovered loot does not disappear when the player looks or moves away. The debug fog reveal fills the interior history, so gated loot, traps and effects appear with it; void remains capped.

Closed-door x-ray silhouettes appear only while the door is hidden (occluded, or in view on still-unexplored space; the fog's soft fringe over a door's explored face does not count), nearby and adjacent to revealed space. Unopened treasure chests use a golden occlusion silhouette on discovered tiles, controlled by the loot X-ray toggle; opening a chest or restoring it as opened removes the highlight. Both silhouettes draw above the fog, so walls still under fog do not hide them; their discovery gates keep them from revealing unexplored space. [Door access](door-access.md) adds concealed wall entrances, locks and colored key markers. Concealed doors expose no silhouette/prompt/marker; discovering a secret reveals a closed door, and opening it permits ordinary sight checks. Door access and explored samples persist independently; `Level.CaptureExploration` owns doors, traps and samples together. `SaveGame.CurrentVersion` rejects earlier development saves; no conversion is attempted.

## Reusable room arrangements

Ordinary rooms combine generated layouts with small authored arrangements: a table and chairs, a barrel cluster, or other reusable furnishings. Complete authored rooms supply landmarks and deliberate gameplay spaces. Standard-room selection currently favors procedural rooms; entrances, exits and special rooms remain authored.

Arrangement definitions select an editable scene, eligible room purposes, selection weight, repeat limit and wall/interior placement preferences. Pools separately bound floor furnishing density and wall mount counts. Compositions rotate together in quarter-turns. The general garrison arrangement pool remains available for generic rooms and contains broken and feasting banquet tables, barrel and stacked crate caches, a dining table with chairs, rubble mounds, mounted torches, banners, and wall-mounted heraldic shields; dining tables exclude sewer-purpose rooms. Mounted torches and banners demonstrate full scenes, including local light and particles.

**Every remaining free area must stay connected, and every active opening must remain accessible.** Placement accounts for actor width, existing obstacles and previously accepted arrangements. Door-to-door connectivity alone is insufficient: isolated pockets are rejected too. Later generated chests must preserve the same rule. Optional furnishings are omitted when they cannot fit safely. The conservative bounds can reject physically possible placements; actual movement, especially for large actors, still needs playtesting.

Gameplay furnishes procedural rooms after their walls and openings are resolved, before navigation baking. Furniture is reproducible from the level seed without consuming layout, encounter or loot randomness. Authored rooms keep their manually composed content. The built-in room generator can also furnish and save editable room scenes through the same placement policy.

See the [arrangement authoring guide](../scenes/levels/arrangements/README.md) for Inspector settings, editor commands and the review playground. Each scene explicitly binds a Content hierarchy and a static placement envelope. Enabled solid collision determines walkable space; light and particle extents do not reserve floor space. Wall compositions require a real supporting span at their configured height and rotate together. Corridor placement patterns remain [future work](development-roadmap.md#room-variety-and-furnishing).
