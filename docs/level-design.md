# Level design

Implemented generation, room authoring, encounters and visibility. [Dungeon layout, access and discovery](dungeon-exploration-design.md) owns intended realm pools, recipes, locks and secrets; [door access](door-access.md) describes implemented locks/secrets and the [progression plan](specs/level-progression-prototype-plan.md) retains future realm recipes and seals.

See the [folder guide](../scenes/levels/README.md) for code and preset locations, and the [room generation harness](../scenes/debug/README.md#room-generation) for single-room and full-floor previews.

## Current generation

Every room is assembled; there are no hand-authored rooms. The [room pool](../scenes/levels/rooms/room_pool.tres) catalogs every room, entrance and exit. A [`Realm`](../scenes/levels/realms/) is one act of the run: a band of five depths, its theme, its corridor tiles (a floor material and a wall style, so corridors match its rooms), the floor plans its floors build from, its boss floor and the [shops](#shops) on its floors. The generator uses the realm covering its depth, and the deepest realm carries on below the last band. Room types, wall and floor styles and furnishing steps carry `Realms` tags naming the **themes** they belong to: a realm assembles only the content tagged with its `Theme`, each room with the styles and steps tagged for that theme (untagged content suits every theme). Realms that share a look share a theme, and a room shared by several themes changes with them: the prison is a Garrison prison or a Sewer lock-up with slimy walls and brick floors. All realms share the dungeon mesh libraries; each draws a different subset of the KayKit and [Asset Studio tiles](../asset-studio/tilesets/README.md) in them.

| Depths | Realm | Theme | Floor plans, most common first | Boss floor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–5 | Garrison | garrison | cluster, hallway | depth 5: the Forsaken Warden |
| 6–10 | Prison | garrison | prison, cluster, hallway | depth 10: the Minotaur |
| 11–15 | Sewer | sewer | large cluster, cluster | depth 15: the Gelatinous Cube |
| 16–20 | Mines | jungle | mines, large cluster | — |
| 21–25 | Cathedral | garrison | cathedral and cloister, cluster | — |

| Theme | Its own rooms | Shared rooms | Corridors |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| garrison | hall, mess hall, barracks, library, alchemist, passage, fenced walkway, chapel, kitchen, guardroom, crypt, graveyard, root cellar | prison, storage, partitioned storage, ruins, entrance, exit | KayKit dirt, stone/arched/gated walls, passageways, torches & heraldic decor |
| sewer | cistern hall, sewer vault, drain tunnel, junction, rat warren | prison, storage, partitioned storage, ruins, entrance, exit | studio bricks, slimy/gated walls, stone/scaffold passageways, torches & shackles |
| jungle | timbered mine, ore chamber, foreman's hut, cavern, crystal cavern, root passage, temple hall, ruined shrine | ruins, entrance, exit | studio earth, cave rock walls & doorways, rail tracks, parked carts, timber/cave passageways, mine lanterns, tool racks, ore veins & glowing crystal clusters |

Every realm's last floor is its boss floor, built from the realm's `BossProfile` ([boss.tres](../scenes/levels/generators/floor_profiles/boss.tres)) when its encounter plan holds a gatekeeper; only the Garrison, the Prison and the Sewer have one so far, so the other realms' last floors build from their ordinary plans. Every other floor draws one of the realm's floor plans by weight, the same plan on every attempt of that floor. The generator's `UniqueFloorPlans` add plans a run meets exactly once: the run seed places the [labyrinth](../scenes/levels/generators/floor_profiles/labyrinth.tres) on one ordinary floor (no gatekeeper, no shop) between depths 7 and 19. A generator `Profile`, when assigned, pins every floor to one plan for tests, the room harness and previews.

A realm schedules its rooms across its floors (`Realm.Rooms`; each realm's arc is in [realm content](realm-content.md)). Each ordinary room has a window of floors (1 is the realm's first), so rooms come in late or retire early: the Garrison's barracks and kitchens fill its upper floors, its crypts and ruins its lower ones, and the Prison shares the Garrison's look but none of its libraries, chapels or tombs. Every floor has one headline: the shop on the first floor after a gatekeeper, the gatekeeper on its floor, or a special room drawn from the realm's headline candidates whose windows span the floor. The run seed alone picks them, so every floor of a run agrees, a candidate headlines at most once per realm, and runs rotate which floor gets the library, the chapel or the graveyard. Layouts place the headline before their other rooms and never draw it again; one that does not fit after a few tries leaves the floor without it. `MaxPerFloor` still caps a room's copies. Each floor plan has a window too: the cloister holds the Cathedral's upper floors and the cathedral plan its lower ones. A realm also puts motifs on some floors (`Realm.Motifs`), drawn per run like headlines: the Garrison's `undead` floors (one or two of floors 3–4, and the Warden's) bring its crypts, the graveyard and bones in its guardrooms and passages, `decay` crumbles floors 4–5, the Prison's `warband` floors 3–5 bring barracks, mess halls and weapon racks, the Sewer's `flooded` floors pool its junctions and vaults, the Mines' `crystals` floors 3–5 grow crystals in caverns and ore chambers, and the Cathedral's `vampire` floors 4–5 open its crypts and hang its chapels in red. Rooms (`RealmRoom.Motif`) and furnishing steps (`FurnishingStep.Motifs`) that name a motif appear only on floors carrying it. Every open floor has at least two headline candidates: the Garrison's great hall, training yard, library, alchemist, chapel, graveyard and treasury; the Prison's warden's office, interrogation room, execution yard, fighting pit and treasury; the Sewer's smugglers' den, cistern hall, outfall chamber, rat king's nest and sunken shrine; the Mines' mine shaft, smelter, crystal cavern and temple hall; and the Cathedral's library, baptistry, bell tower, reliquary hall, throne room, banquet hall and vampire's crypt. A realm without a schedule (test fixtures, previews) offers every catalog room of its theme.

The entrance and exit carry the stairs. Encounters do not follow realms: the [encounter prototype](#first-realm-encounter-prototype) covers depths 1–5 and later depths use its development fallback. Per-depth room roles and required routes remain proposed.

Ordinary room recipes range from compact to large, while cells, closets and plan rooms stay small; a realm's pool keeps at least 15% compact and 25% medium rooms beside its larger ones. Sizes and minimum floor areas are authored per recipe, and furnishing strategies keep their required identity while packing optional content into the available space. Logical tiles are 4×4 world units; the room harness reports both the bounding dimensions and footprint area for non-rectangular shapes.

Generation packs and rotates rooms around an entrance, prefers a geometrically distant exit, connects every doorway, walls corridors, records room entrances, builds floor reachability, decides special encounters, assigns validated lock/key pairs, stands the realm's keepers, then places other loot and builds the navigation grid. Ordinary encounters use the completed geometry. Ordinary connectivity and a distant exit do not guarantee a mandatory route or encounter order.

Boss floors build a linear spine of rooms from the entrance to the [boss_exit](../scenes/levels/rooms/room_groups/boss_exit.tres) group, whose boss room is assembled with the gatekeeper's minimum clearance and is the only way into the descent's room behind it. Corridors join exactly the doorways the layout paired, and generation retries any layout where the descent is reachable around the boss room.

Generation hands gameplay a `GeneratedFloor` (`Level.Floor`): the layout, rooms, doors, arrival, descent and encounter plan, plus every spatial query gameplay asks (room lookup, footprint clearance, free spawn and landing positions). The generator runs its own placement checks against the same instance, and freezes it at handoff. Components read seed, depth, floor, run and exploration from their `Level`, never from the generator or the session.

Generation settings and factories are assigned in [level.tscn](../scenes/levels/level.tscn). [Randomness and seeds](randomness-and-seeds.md) owns reproducibility and saved differences.

## First-realm encounter prototype

The first five depths use the [Sunken Garrison encounter resource](../scenes/levels/encounters/sunken_garrison.tres). Weighted, depth-bounded entries are **packs**: monsters that spawn together. Floors spend a budget that the level's [threat profile](../scenes/levels/encounters/threat_profile.tres) gives each depth; a pack costs its members' threat-level costs times `(N + 1) / 2`, because members fighting together outlast being fought one at a time (see the [balance model](specs/balance-model.md#floors-and-gatekeepers)). The level owns the profile, which also sets the XP monsters pay, and generation passes it to the factory, so realm encounter resources never name it. Each pack's first member takes a spawn point spaced from every other pack, and the rest stand within 3.5 m of it in the same room; a member without space is dropped. Realm 1 opens with rat packs, brings rat hordes from depth 2 with occasional skeletons, and mixes skeleton groups from depth 3 to its gatekeeper floor at depth 5. Its fallback is the [Prison encounter resource](../scenes/levels/encounters/prison_encounters.tres) for depths 6–10: swarms and flocks of giant bats, lone thieving nymphs, orc grunts alone, in pairs and from depth 8 in threes, and raiders (a grunt with a nymph). Its fallback in turn is the [Sewer encounter resource](../scenes/levels/encounters/sewer_encounters.tres) for depths 11–15: packs and hordes of sewer rats (the plague rat at Sewer strength), acid blobs alone or in twos and threes, lone or paired snakes, and a spitting slime keeping watch alone. The [Mines encounter resource](../scenes/levels/encounters/mines_encounters.tres) follows for depths 16–20: stone golems, which guard their rooms, alone, with two giant bats or from depth 18 in pairs, with swarms of giant bats and lone or paired snakes standing in for the cave dwellers the realm still lacks. Each realm's second floor (depths 7, 12 and 17) places two nests: [bat roosts](../scenes/levels/encounters/bat_roost.tscn) that release giant bats in the Prison and the Mines and [slime vats](../scenes/levels/encounters/slime_vat.tscn) that release acid blobs in the Sewer. The Prison ends on its gatekeeper, the Minotaur, on depth 10, and the Sewer on the Gelatinous Cube on depth 15; the Mines have none yet (`HasGatekeeper` off), so their last floor builds from its ordinary plans with an open descent. From the Prison on, each placed chest may instead be a chest mimic waiting in its place (`RealmEncounterFactory.MimicChance`), which takes the chest's persistent ID. The factory's encounter plan substitutes a nest or the gatekeeper for a whole pack, so budgets alone do not measure final floor difficulty. [Monster definitions](monster-catalog/README.md) supply fixed strength. Deeper depths use an explicit development fallback, not finished realm content.

- Depth 1 introduces ordinary skeleton encounters.
- Depth 2 adds rats and finite nests. Nests activate in revealed nearby space, limit simultaneous summons and total slots, and never refill killed slots. Destroying a nest stops further summons while already-released minions remain active.
- Depth 5 adds the Forsaken Warden. Defeat unlocks downward travel through an objective check. Visible seals, boss phases and guaranteed reward-room topology are not implemented.

A room may provide a `GatekeeperSpawnMarker` to control boss position and facing after rotation. Author one intended anchor and ensure its physical clearance. Otherwise generation picks the roomiest supported location nearest the descent, outside the arrival's room and, on a floor with a boss room, in the boss room or the descent's room behind it, and retries a bounded number of deterministic layouts; failure returns safely instead of loading an invalid floor. The site is decided once, before locks and loot: locks never seal its room, loot keeps clear of its minimum footprint, and the gatekeeper takes the first encounter slot there. Large-actor movement retains its clearance constraints, and tall enemies stay hidden until their tile is discovered.

Cleared enemies, destroyed nests, killed summon slots and Warden defeat persist across revisits. Living actors reset to their generated initial state; partial health, position and summon timers are not saved. See [monster authoring](monster-authoring-design.md) for tactics and the [roadmap](development-roadmap.md#first-three-floor-acceptance) for playtest acceptance.

## Fountains

The generator's `Fountains` profile ([fountain_profile.tres](../scenes/props/fountains/fountain_profile.tres)) schedules health and mana fountains across the whole run. Each `FountainKind` draws two or three fountains from the run seed alone, so every floor agrees without shared state. Depths 2 to 24 split into equal bands, one fountain per band, and kinds avoid sharing a floor. A floor's fountain uses that kind's stream to pick one of its four designs and a free-standing room tile. Tiles walkable on every side are preferred, connector-adjacent tiles are skipped, and the placement keeps loot spacing and arrival clearance. Like chests, a fountain is a solid navmesh obstacle that must stand on reachable floor, keep door swings clear and leave no inaccessible pocket. A cramped floor that cannot fit a fountain logs it and the run has one fewer.

`Fountain` grants its `FountainBlessing` strategy (`RestoreHealthBlessing`, `RestoreManaBlessing`) once, through the ordinary timed interaction. A drinker whose pool is full sees a blocked prompt. Drinking hides the art's `Liquid` node, leaving the basin empty. The [pool effects](world-effects.md#fountain-effects) spray a burst over the change, and the drinker plays the healing or mana burst. The drink also plays the drinker's use-item gesture: an interaction offer can name an animation action, and `Player.CurrentActionId` falls back to it while the interaction runs. The fountain's `depth:N:fountain:<kind>` id becomes one of the run's cleared entities, so a revisited or loaded floor rebuilds it drained.

## Shops

A realm's `Shops` stand keepers on the realm floors their placements name, as solid props placed after locks and keys and before loot. A layout with no spot for a keeper is retried. [Shops](shops.md#placement) owns the placement rules.

## Loot

Most of a floor's loot lies waiting to be explored, as in Rogue and Shattered Pixel Dungeon, rather than dropping from kills. `MapGenerator.Loot.cs` places, after the fountains:

- **Reward chests.** Every room behind a generated lock (except a locked exit) holds a gold chest ([chest_gold.tscn](../scenes/props/chest_gold.tscn), the uncommon table, two or three rolls). Every room reached only through secret doors holds a secret chest ([chest_secret.tscn](../scenes/props/chest_secret.tscn), the rare table, two or three rolls). Neither is ever a mimic. A room too furnished for a chest gets the chest's loot on its floor instead.
- **Chests:** one to three per floor against walls, each one roll of the common chest table; from the Prison on, each may be a mimic.
- **Loose items:** three to five spots, each one roll of the same table.
- **Gold piles:** two to three, of 20 + 6 gold per depth, varied by 40% either way.

Each spot is its own seeded reward source. [Stats and itemization](stats-and-itemization.md#loot-eligibility) describes the tables.

## Room geometry and stairs

Every room is a `Room` with floor, wall and decoration GridMaps built by its room type. GridMap cells are one world unit with centering disabled. Logical map tiles are **4×4 units centered on their anchor**, even when floor meshes are smaller or larger. The room owns its doorways completely: assembly hands the room its tile map as data (footprint tiles as floor, stairwell and pool pits as chasms, each doorway as a connector facing outward) and fills every doorway right away with its wall style's door, its opening frame or nothing (an open walkway); every other edge is walled. Nothing re-derives the map from geometry, and nothing adds, swaps or removes a doorway's door later: the room hands each door to the floor with the doorway it fills, and a doorway the corridors cannot reach fails generation. Before furnishing, assembly rejects a candidate when its inferred wall regions overlap. MeshLibrary item transforms are included; compound wall regions follow the standard junction footprints. The generator rotates complete rooms in quarter-turns: GridMaps, props and doors as geometry, the tile map as data. Room and floor wall generation resolve edge junctions with corners, T pieces and crossings, retaining compatible straight wall variants. If corridor walls cannot fit around a room-owned divider, generation retries the layout without committing overlapping pieces. Door frames and secret disguises reserve their 4×1 footprint; 1.5-unit wall pieces fit adjoining perpendicular walls against the frame face. Frames add no permanent collision, keeping the full doorway clear when the door is open. Assets must conform to their structural footprints and seam positions; see the [remaining wall-piece plan](specs/exact-wall-placement-plan.md).

The level's stairs are scenes ([up](../scenes/levels/stairs/stairs_up.tscn), [down](../scenes/levels/stairs/stairs_down.tscn)) placed by the entrance and exit room types' `StairsStep`: the staircase with its collision, the arrival `PlayerSpawnPoint` and the `LevelTransitionTrigger`. Up-stairs sit flush against the wall directly behind their top, including on wall runs with deeper pillars elsewhere. Down-stairs sink into a two-tile stairwell cut into open floor with floor on every side. Continuous stone walls line both sides one floor below, with their tops flush with the floor and the original fence above. The walk-on end and the exit at the bottom remain open. The room maps it as an enclosed chasm. Either way the landing at the walk-on end (the up-stairs' foot, the down-stairs' top, flush with the floor) is claimed for the rest of the assembly: no floor prop may cover it and every doorway must keep reaching it. Generated loot, keys and traps keep clear of every landing, and floor reachability floods from the arrival's `Landing`.

Corridor floor materials and wall styles validate before room generation. Missing assignments or invalid tiles report a generation failure and return to the menu, clearing the transition fade.

## Room assembly

Garrison rooms are assembled with their content by a `RoomType` ([room types](../scenes/levels/rooms/room_types/)): one strategy, or weighted palette of strategies, per axis. `RoomAssembler` runs them in a fixed order — shape → doorways → walls → circulation → columns → furnishing steps → floor → props — each axis on its own derived stream, so changing a floor style or toggling columns never moves furniture. Rooms are finished before floor packing and rotate as a whole; gameplay must connect every doorway, as before. A required step that cannot fit retries with a new shape (`Attempts`); a type that never fits throws instead of delivering a different room, naming the step and the last placement it refused. The [authoring guide](../scenes/levels/generators/assembly/README.md) covers adding props, groups, styles, steps and room types.

| Axis | Strategies |
| --- | --- |
| Shape and size | `RectangleShape` (optional odd width), `LShape`, `TShape`, `UShape`, `CrossShape`; width across, length along the room axis (+Z) |
| Doorways | `MinDoorways`/`MaxDoorways`, `DoorwayPlacement` (any, long-axis ends, long sides, a single end that leaves the far wall for a focal point), `SecretDoorChance` per doorway (a secret always creates a door), `SecretDetection`, `DoorChance` for the rest (1 always, 0 never), then `FrameChance` for the wall style's opening frame; the rest are open walkways |
| Circulation | Doorway approaches or a door-to-door spine kept free of furniture |
| Walls | `WallStyle`: weighted straight pieces and accents, plus doorway `DoorScene` and `Opening`, and its family's structural pieces (`Half`, `Short`, `Corner`, `TSplit`, `Crossing`, KayKit's by default; cave walls bring their own). The boundary graph places those junction pieces and retains a chosen straight variant when its footprint fits. Wider accents and pillars are kept when their footprints do not intersect other pieces; otherwise they give way to the style's first plain straight piece |
| Floor | `UniformFloor`, `BorderedFloor`, `SplitFloor` over `FloorMaterial`s (weighted slabs, quarter pieces and wear accents); a split runs one straight or diagonal boundary across the room, never scattered patches; `FloorInlay`s set drains, gutters and water channels into the tiling; an optional `FloorRug` lays a runner down the middle or a bordered rug over the open floor, without collision, so spawns and loot still use its tiles |
| Columns | `AisleColumns`, `PerimeterColumns`, `CenterColumns`, `CornerColumns`, behind `ColumnChance`; symmetric groups place all-or-nothing |
| Furnishing | `AlongWallsStep`, `CornersStep`, `RowsStep`, `CenterStep`, `ScatterStep`, `WallDecorStep`, `CellsStep` (barred cells with gates), `FencedAreaStep` (bays, walkways, dividers), `ArcadeStep` (rows of arches), `PoolStep` (water sunk into the floor) and `ChoiceStep` (one weighted variant); a step with `MinimumCount` is required, `Chance` makes it occasional and `Uniform` keeps one content choice per room (one banner colour) |

Steps place `Placeable`s. A `Prop` is one object: mesh-library items (cosmetic variants) or scenes for props that need nodes, measured from the model. Its front faces local +Z; back-to-wall props stand with -Z on the face of the wall pieces behind them, wall props hang on the mounting surface at `MountHeight`. That surface is measured from the wall's band above 0.7 m, so base rubble, roots and plinths never push decor off the wall. Along the wall, decor hangs only inside the **mount spans** the wall models declare (exported by the Asset Studio, read by `WallFootprints.MeasureEdge`): cave walls declare the bays between their timber posts, so `WallDecorStep` and the corridor steps center decor on each bay and never across a post. Walls that declare none take decor along their whole length. Rooms hang their decor before the floor walls its corridors, and where a corridor wall meets a room's wall the floor's junction takes over half of the room's edge, which may stand a post behind a prop; once the floor's walls are built, `MapGenerator` takes down every room wall prop no longer inside its wall's mount spans. A `PropGroup` is an anchor plus optional members at offsets (a bed with nightstand and chest, a barrel with a side table); `SeatedTable` spaces one kind of seat along a measured table (chairs or benches, spaced by their width). Every piece is validated on its own, so a member that does not fit is dropped, and pieces may rest on others (plates and candles on a table). Weighted picks are balanced over the room: counts follow the weights, while equal options alternate instead of repeating; unused variants come first. Loose props carry a small seeded turn and shift (`TurnJitter`, `ShiftJitter`) so barrels, stools, vases and tableware sit naturally off the packing grid.

`RoomBlueprint` checks every piece for floor support, wall and prop overlap (with each prop's `Gap`) and circulation, and keeps all doorways mutually reachable on a 0.5-unit `WalkabilityGrid` sized for the largest ordinary walking body (`RoomAssembler.ActorRadius`, 0.6, the skeletons; the player's 0.5 capsule fits wherever they do). Inaccessible pockets behind furniture are allowed up to the type's `MaxPocketShare` of standable floor (default 10%). Interactive props reserve the space in front of them. Walkability tests nine sample points per cell (a quarter-cell lattice) against the full actor radius, so a gap is open exactly when the actor fits through it, wherever the grid falls: a 2.4-unit gate admits a 2-unit actor, a 1.7-unit one does not. Assembled props are plain nodes under the room's `Props` (mesh plus static collision on the prop layer, so they block actors and navigation but not exploration sight). Stretched structural pieces such as cell bars get box colliders sized to their stretch. Prison gates are ordinary doors with [interior identities](door-access.md#interior-cell-gates).

Fitted structure can mark room-local floor patches for the later cosmetic pass. The shared prison recipe uses `CellsStep.CellFloors` to choose brown or gray dirt for all its cell rows, independently of the stone or brick aisle. Patches align to the quarter-tile grid and use a separate cosmetic RNG stream. Patches do not change furniture placement or walkability. The [room generation harness](../scenes/debug/README.md#room-generation) previews the production prison, depot and treasury alongside the other realm rooms. The Garrison treasury has one entrance, secret 80% of the time, and a chest containing 240 gold.

Arcades divide rooms without closing them off. `ArcadeStep` stands rows of arches wall to wall: the sewer vault crosses its length with a row every three tiles, cutting it into vaulted bays, and chapels and cistern halls raise a pair of arcades along their sides when no colonnade or pool edge is in the way. Each arch is KayKit's open doorway frame, a 2 m opening between 1 m jambs; the jambs and the piers that close a row to the walls block bodies and projectiles, so a fight moves through the openings, and every bay stays reachable.

Water is impassable to the player and walking monsters. `PoolStep` cuts whole tiles out of the floor and fills them with pool pieces: quay walls around the perimeter, open water between tiles. Scattered pools keep a dry border; cistern halls always have a centered pool with dry routes around it. Drain tunnels keep a dry central walkway and crosswalks at both ends, with water extending to the room walls on the left, right, or both sides. Every layout preserves doorway connectivity. Flying traversal is not implemented or verified.

The room maps water as chasm tiles, so it reveals with the room but cannot host walking actors, loot, or traps. Actor-only barriers on the `walking_terrain` physics layer block entry even without fencing; walking actor scenes include that layer in their masks, while projectile and sight queries ignore it. Monster navigation treats the pits as off the floor. Optional railings stand along dry banks and are omitted where they would obstruct circulation. Walkable floor inlays use grates rather than open-water visuals. Every water surface is drawn by the [water shader](../scenes/levels/dungeon/water/water.gdshader): translucent at the surface, murky within about a unit of depth, pale where it meets stone or floating things.

### Corridor styling and decoration

Each realm's `DungeonTileFactory` pairs a `FloorMaterial` and `WallStyle` with an ordered array of `CorridorStep` strategies (`CorridorPassagewayStep`, `CorridorThresholdStep`, `CorridorCornerStep`, `CorridorHallwayStep`, and in the Mines `CorridorRailStep` and `CorridorDeadEndStep`). The floor's boundary pass uses the corridor wall style's structural pieces. Compatible room and corridor wall variants survive it when their occupied footprints fit; conflicting variants fall back to the corridor style's plain straight piece, and straight and half pieces face inward (`WallFootprints.FacingInto`). After boundary walls are placed, `CorridorBlueprint` measures decoratable straight wall runs (excluding accents, corners, doorways and concealed secret-door walls), doorway thresholds, L-corners, T-junctions, dead ends (one-way tiles clear of doors and connectors, so a tile before a secret door is not one) and straight 1-tile-wide spans, then executes each `CorridorStep` on its own derived decoration RNG stream and emits the accepted props under `CorridorProps`.

Mine rails lay connected runs of straight, curved and buffer-stop pieces: from a dead end back up its shaft, or along a straight span. A run is planned and checked tile by tile before any piece goes down, and laid in order up to the first piece that no longer fits, so tracks never have gaps. Buffer stops only end runs on dead ends, and a cart may park on a laid one. Timber frames and cave passageways are structural, so they may stand over walk-over track. Dead-end props (carts, ore piles) then fill shafts the rails left free.

### Lighting and atmosphere

Every playing world shares `world_lighting.tscn` and `game_environment.tres`: cool ambient fill, a subdued reflection sky, warm local fixtures, contact occlusion and restrained height haze. A realm whose walls and floors read darker than the rest scales and tints that fill on its floors (`Realm.AmbientScale`, `Realm.AmbientTint`): the Mines' earth and timber get a brighter, neutral fill, since the cool one leaves them murky. The sky supplies material reflections without appearing as the dungeon background. An equipped torch or lamp enables the hero's forward point light. On normal floors, a separate overhead fill keeps the hero readable under ambient and fixture lighting. Darkness disables ambient light, sky reflections, haze and fixture flames.

The player's `Normal`, `Lit` and `Dark` profiles live beside `player_light.tscn`. An equipped light uses the `Normal` profile on ordinary floors and `Lit` on dark floors, with wall and door clamping in both. Without a torch or lamp, the `Dark` profile supplies soft, shadowless fill from above the hero. The [darkness screen effect](screen-effects.md) fades that view to black around the hero. Fog-of-war reveal remains independent of lighting.

`DungeonSurfaceFinish` applies the Inspector-authored `SurfaceFinishProfile` resources under `scenes/levels/lighting/surface_finishes/` to private copies of the generator's mesh libraries before generation. The first matching item-name prefix selects cloth, damp stone, wood or dry stone response. This changes roughness and specular response only: tile geometry, IDs, collision and source resources are preserved, as are authored metal, deliberate gloss and custom shaders. Finishes do not affect generation randomness or placement.

Torch flames carry sparse, softly fading dust motes. Their parent flame's darkness and exploration visibility hide the motes with the fixture. Mounted torch meshes favor their fine LOD throughout the gameplay zoom range. Equal subdivisions in the positional shadow atlas give fixtures a consistent shadow resolution as the camera moves. The shared rendering settings use 4× MSAA, debanding, filtered shadows and high-quality half-resolution contact occlusion. Volumetric fog uses an 80-pixel base volume with 96 depth slices over 35 metres, covering the current camera's zoom range. The shorter range retains approximately the previous depth detail for low floor mist. Volume resolution lives under **Project Settings → Rendering → Environment**; fog length and SSAO radius, intensity and power remain in `game_environment.tres`. SSAO renders at full resolution with one blur pass and a short radius, so contact shading stays crisp instead of spreading soft halos. Revisit fog length if changing camera distance, pitch or maximum zoom.

Gameplay focus uses `focus_blur_effect.tscn` on the existing `ScreenEffects` layer, before status overlays and below the HUD. Its shader keeps a central ellipse sharp, smoothly increasing blur toward every edge regardless of camera depth or rotation. Edit **FocusBlur → Intensity** to scale or disable it, and **Surface → Material → Shader Parameters** for the center, clear radii, falloff and maximum filter width. The radii are viewport fractions: zooming out protects more world space. Each instance owns its material and back-buffer copy. Gameplay camera depth of field stays disabled; lighting and HUD sharpness are unchanged.

Each `Realm.Atmosphere` selects an Inspector-authored `AtmosphereProfile` under `scenes/levels/lighting/atmosphere_profiles/`. Its exported `Effects` collection selects scenes rooted in `AtmosphereEffect`: haze, any number of particle layers, or other floor effects. Empty collections, haze-only and particle-only presets are valid. `DungeonAtmosphere` owns their floor lifetime and shared darkness gate; effects validate only their own dependencies. Shared floor context supplies lazily cached playable-surface masks, bounds and deterministic emission fields. All randomness stays in the cosmetic domain and is independent of effect order, so composition changes neither layout nor saved gameplay state. Darkness suspends effects unless their exported `ActiveInDarkness` permits them; clearing or regenerating a floor releases their references before the shared textures.

**Debug → Visual effects** independently toggles fog, ambient occlusion, focus blur, surface marks, drips/splashes, atmospheric dust/glimmer, wind and reflections. World-local overrides live in `WorldLighting.DisabledEffects` and survive floor regeneration in that world without modifying authored resources. Each `AtmosphereEffect.Category` opts its scene into a switch; `None` leaves an effect independent. Disabled layers hide and suspend processing, including the haze clock and puddle sheen. Wind restores static prop materials and stops ambient motion, including its animated shadow redraws. The reflections switch disables sky reflections, local probe rendering/recaptures and any authored screen-space reflections; direct-light specular highlights still work. For performance comparisons, keep camera, floor and resolution fixed, compare each effect alone and together, and measure frame milliseconds: a 120 FPS cap can hide gains above the display limit. Probe recaptures should also be checked during movement.

Torch flames also carry faint expanding smoke, inheriting their fixture's existing darkness and discovery gates. The normal hero helper lights do not illuminate volumetric haze, preserving outward scene shadows without adding a travelling luminous cloud. Global volumetric density stays zero; only the floor's masked haze contributes. Temporal reprojection is disabled to keep moving lights and discovery boundaries from leaving fog trails.

`AtmosphereHaze` exports its `Volume`, `Height`, `Animate` and `Enabled` options. Color, density, ambient scattering, noise scale, world-space drift and wobble amplitude/period are authored directly in the volume's native `ShaderMaterial` parameters. Its local clock pauses with the world and stops when disabled or dark. Broad density patterns retain soft gaps through the mist’s height, keeping their movement readable along the view ray. The effect uses a private runtime material and the shared exploration coverage, so mist cannot expose hidden rooms.

`AtmosphereParticles` is the same reusable implementation for dust, glowing motes and other authored particle scenes. It exports density, particle budget, minimum/maximum height, emission samples, tile inset and an optional stable `SeedKey`; particle placement does not depend on haze. Native `GPUParticles3D`, `ParticleProcessMaterial`, draw mesh and shader settings remain the Inspector controls for color, size, speed, lifetime, turbulence and light response. Runtime emission changes use a private process material. Disabled or zero-population layers do not generate emission textures or simulate.

Current presets retain dry lit dust, cool mist and humid air with softly pulsing motes. Dust scatters direct, shadowed light through independently tumbling grain orientations, fading edge-on and glinting when aligned; it has no emission or ambient glow. The normal hero light excludes the effects layer through its profile's `LightLayers`, while dark-floor carried light retains its broader rendering layers. Glowing motes use an emissive material without adding lights to the environment.


The garrison, jungle and sewer presets select the shared `drips.tscn` effect or an inherited intensity preset. Garrison uses 0.35 particles per tile, jungle 0.85, and sewer 1.5 with a shorter cycle and a 3072-particle cap. Drops and rings occupy 80% of their cycle; drips continue on dark floors. The effect uses the existing particle host with `DryFloorOnly`. Its particle material sets the cycle duration; `drip_splash.gdshader` animates an accelerating drop followed by an expanding ring at the same dry-floor point. Shader parameters control height, size and tint; the host controls density and seed. Splash rings project onto nearby opaque upward-facing surfaces using scene depth; `surface_range` limits their vertical reach. The drops remain cosmetic, without collision or water impacts. Shared water shading adds world-space moving normals and irregular drifting light patches; tune wave scale/speed/strength, caustic strength and roughness in `dungeon/water/water.tres`. The light patterns are stylized surface shading rather than projected lighting on nearby walls.

Surface weathering uses native `SurfaceMark` decals with shared `SurfaceMarkProfile` resources under `scenes/effects/surface_marks/`. Profiles expose tint, color blending, optional roughness, and a choice of authored mask variants or procedural edges. Mark scenes inherit the shared `surface_mark.tscn` receiver and distance fade and can override projection size and placement. Derived albedo/ORM textures are cached per profile, with Inspector edits updating subscribed marks. Wet marks darken surfaces and use low-roughness opaque centers with soft organic edges. Tint alpha controls ORM coverage independently of `AlbedoMix`, so darkening can remain restrained while wet cores become glossy. Scuffs and dried blood use color alone and share the drag masks with different tints. Each generated layer cycles through its masks from a seeded starting point.

The level owns one `LocalReflectionProbe`, with its focus explicitly supplied by `Main` on player spawn. In once-update mode it captures nearby world geometry after at least 6 metres of travel and a 1.5-second cooldown; focus, capture offset, distance threshold, cooldown and native probe bounds/intensity are exported. Box projection supplies approximate local-room reflections for wet decals and other glossy materials. Capture excludes character/effect render layers and switches off with world darkness. This is a throttled cubemap reflection, not a live planar mirror or screen-space reflection.

`AtmosphereSurfaceMarks` scatters persistent wet patches among the same potential dry-floor drip sites: garrison uses sparse small marks, jungle moderate marks and sewer denser larger patches. Density, cap, size range, seed and sampling are exported. Placement is cosmetic, independent of generation/loot, and remains on dark floors. Marks indicate existing dampness rather than accumulating dynamically with individual drops. Mounted torches and hearths author soot on their wall-facing side; braziers author a floor scorch. Soot belongs to the fixture scene and persists when the light is off. No prop IDs choose appearance or behavior. To add another stain, duplicate a profile and mark scene, then attach it to a prop or select it in an atmosphere mark layer.

The prison and mines presets use seeded scuffs along dry corridors and inside ordinary doorway thresholds. Prison splatters are limited to cells, cell blocks and interrogation rooms. Drag marks point toward ordinary entrances in cell blocks and interrogation rooms. `AtmosphereSurfaceMarks` can follow open passages, restrict a layer by room type, and cap marks both per room and per floor. The mines use a low-count layer of dust falling from ceiling height, and `AtmosphereWallDust` lets dust roll down a wall every few seconds: it picks a wall near the middle of the view whose face the camera sees and plays Tremor's pooled `scenes/effects/wall_dust.tscn` burst there. Like the spell's dust it is unshaded, so it rests while the floor is dark. These effects are available on dark floors and follow the existing surface mark and dust switches.

Actors can leave marks too: a `TrailMarksComponent` drops its mark scene every `Spacing` meters while its `MovementComponent` moves the actor over revealed floor, holds each mark for `Lifetime`, then fades it out. It recycles a fixed ring of `Capacity` marks, so a long chase never adds nodes. The acid blob leaves `slime_mark.tscn`, a translucent green wet profile. Trail marks are cosmetic and use the same surface-mark switch.

To author another atmosphere, create an `AtmosphereProfile` and choose its `Effects` scenes. Duplicate or inherit a scene under `atmosphere_effects/` and adjust its exported and native material settings; select it in any realm preset. New rendering behavior implements the small `AtmosphereEffect` preparation, activation and release contract in its scene root, using the supplied floor context. It needs no new slot or branch in the controller, level or realm.

Selected props also opt into [ambient motion](ambient-motion.md) through `Prop.Motion`. Generated levels and the room-generation harness supply a world-local airflow after placement; banner cloth and ferns retain their original models, placement geometry and collision. Motion and its shadow share a pausable clock, with force and response presets exposed in the Inspector.

Rooms and corridor palettes each expose `FixtureLitChance` (default 85%): generation chooses each fixture's initial state on a separate cosmetic seed, so lighting does not change placement. Dark-floor visibility still follows the environmental-light rules.

Every flickering light shares one Inspector-tunable `FlickerProfile` resource that turns noise into a brightness multiplier. Presets live in `scenes/effects/flicker_profiles/`: `torch` gives wall torches and candles a restrained, smooth wavering, `dim` serves the player's dim light, `flame` serves fire spells and `electric` serves lightning strobes. Each light starts its flicker at its own random offset, so the torches spawned with a floor never flicker in step. Standalone lights are `FlickeringLight` nodes, which flicker around their `Energy` only while visible; lights whose energy a spell effect already drives apply the flicker through `EffectLight`.

## Doors and enemy navigation

Monsters navigate on the floor's `ClearanceGrid`, not a navigation mesh. Once every solid is placed, generation measures the distance to the nearest solid on the same quarter-cell lattice as walkability, from the same tiles and collision outlines (inside the actor height band), and keeps each 0.5-unit cell's roomiest sample. A cell is traversable for a body radius when that clearance covers the whole radius, so one grid serves the rat, the skeletons and the Warden: each goes only where its body capsule physically fits, and steers toward the roomiest point of each cell. Every door, interior cell gates included, is a gate over its shut leaf's footprint; its frame and its open leaf are ordinary solids, so an open door still blocks at its jambs and beside the opening. Closed doors block physics, exploration sight and navigation; barred interior cell gates block traversal while allowing exploration into the cell. Opening or closing a door changes routes on the next query.

Flyers navigate a second grid, `GeneratedFloor.AirClearance`, built from the same walls, void, gates and tall solids. On it pits and water count as floor, and solids no taller than `ClearanceGrid.AirClearanceHeight` (1.2 m) and water's walking barriers do not block, so a flyer crosses pools and low furniture but never walls, shut doors or tall props. Every cell open to walkers is open to flyers. Generation adds the shared solids once and copies that grid, so the second grid costs little. A monster picks its grid with `NavigationComponent.Layer`; the level and the debug arena each keep their grids by layer behind `INavigableWorld.NavigationFor`. A drop with no free spot in throwing range, such as loot from a bat killed over water, lands on the nearest free floor in sight.

The level's `FloorNavigation` computes shared Dijkstra maps on the grid. A chase field per target and body size leads every chaser of that size downhill toward its target. Steps through cells tighter than the body radius plus a `NavigationProfile.PreferredMargin` cost more, so routes keep to the middle of corridors and doorways and round corners instead of cutting them, while open rooms still get direct lines. A flee field derived from it leads away from a threat and out of dead ends. Roaming, searching and ring points use bounded A* routes with the same costs. `NavigationComponent` aims at the farthest cell down the field that it can walk to in a straight line, heads straight for a nearby target once the line is clear, steers apart from other monsters (they never collide physically), and side-steps toward open space before reporting a stall. A monster whose `EnemyBehaviorProfile.StayInHomeRoom` is set (the Warden) moves anywhere in its spawn room but holds at its edge. Fields, routes and reachability are plain C#, so they are tested headless. The debug menu's Navigation overlay draws the grid. [Monster authoring](monster-authoring-design.md#awareness-and-pursuit) owns detection and pursuit rules.

Every walkable floor tile collides as one flat box at a shared height (0.05); surface detail and the slightly higher dirt and rubble tiles are visual only, so bodies never snag on seams between tiles. The player's body is a 0.5-radius capsule matching its model, skeletons 0.6. On stairs, a body pushing into a step with open space one step up is lifted over it, so slim bodies climb the 0.5-unit steps.

Fields are shared: every chaser of a similar body size follows one field per target, computed only as far out as its farthest chaser needs and refreshed when it goes stale or a door changes, so a crowd costs little more than a single monster. A monster makes progress only while its remaining route shrinks, so pacing in place counts as a stall.

Known limitations:

- **One plane:** the grid is 2D, so stairs and pits are not monster terrain; flying or swimming monsters would need their own traversal rule.
- **Static apart from doors:** only doors change the grid at runtime. A destroyed nest keeps its cells blocked until the floor is rebuilt.
- **Low obstacles:** anything rising into the actor height band (above 0.15) blocks every body, so curbs and pool rims stop rats as well.
- **Room-bound Warden:** releasing the Warden is a data change, but its 1.0-radius body is wider than the 1.57 units an open door leaves clear, so it would still pass only doorless openings.

## Map occlusion and fog of war

Permanent black caps hide void under the rotating isometric camera. Gameplay exploration uses bounded omnidirectional line of sight from the player, independent of room-entry events, facing, lighting and camera zoom. Walls, unsanctioned room/corridor edges and closed door thresholds block sight; open doorways reveal only their visible wedge. Exact diagonal corner crossings require both routes to be open. Furnishings do not block discovery in this first implementation. Editor previews expose generated space.

Discovery records an eight-by-eight sample grid per tile. Newly seen samples stay explored permanently, including across travel and save/load; unseen parts of a partially explored tile stay covered. A depth-based screen shader maps rendered surfaces back to this world-aligned history, feathers the boundary inward and fades newly discovered space into view. Feathering does not change authoritative discovery. There is no dimmed remembered-space state or current-line-of-sight requirement for already discovered content.

Exploration belongs to the level's `FloorDiscovery` node ([floor_discovery.tscn](../scenes/levels/floor_discovery.tscn), wired to `Level.Discovery`). `SightRadius` controls reach in world units (default 24), `FadeSeconds` how long newly seen space fades in (default 0.35 seconds) and `FeatherWidth` how far the fog feathers inward (default 1 world unit). Each floor owns its exploration texture and publishes it with the map bounds and fade clock as `fog_*` global shader uniforms ([fog_of_war.gdshaderinc](../scenes/levels/fog_of_war.gdshaderinc)), so the fog and the silhouettes above it share one coverage test. Sight updates at most ten times per second while moving, and refreshes after door changes; a closed door seals its threshold because sight reads the door itself. The fade clock stops once a reveal has faded in. Exploration is independent of normal/dark-floor illumination.

Content gates retain tile-based discovery: discovering any sample on a tile enables its loot, effects and eligible x-ray silhouettes. The existing `ExplorationGateComponent` hides torch visuals, particles and lights until that tile is discovered. Previously discovered loot does not disappear when the player looks or moves away. The debug fog reveal fills the interior history, so gated loot, traps and effects appear with it; void remains capped.

Closed-door x-ray silhouettes appear only while the door is hidden (occluded, or in view on still-unexplored space; the fog's soft fringe over a door's explored face does not count), nearby and adjacent to revealed space. Unopened treasure chests use a golden occlusion silhouette on discovered tiles, controlled by the loot X-ray toggle; opening a chest or restoring it as opened removes the highlight. Door, chest, monster and loot silhouettes all draw above the fog, so walls still under fog do not hide them; their discovery gates keep them from revealing unexplored space. None of them draws over an actor in view: monsters and loot mark their visible pixels in a stencil mask, the hero does the same through its `XrayOccluderComponent`, and every x-ray skips those pixels. A hidden pixel shows only when what hides it is a wall, a closed door or fogged ground ([xray_occlusion.gdshaderinc](../scenes/levels/xray_occlusion.gdshaderinc)): the x-ray reconstructs the nearer surface from the depth buffer and checks it against `floor_wall_mask` and the exploration fog. A projectile, puddle, prop, another actor or the floor in front of something is no reason to see through, so new effects and props never need their own x-ray exception. Without a loaded floor (harnesses and previews) every occluder counts. [Door access](door-access.md) adds concealed wall entrances, locks and colored key markers. Concealed doors expose no silhouette/prompt/marker; discovering a secret reveals a closed door, and opening it permits ordinary sight checks. Door access and explored samples persist independently; `Level.CaptureExploration` owns doors, traps and samples together. `SaveGame.CurrentVersion` rejects earlier development saves; no conversion is attempted.

Actors' visuals stop at walls. On the exploration sample grid (`MapData.WorldBounds`), `FloorDiscovery` also publishes the floor's `WallMask` as `floor_wall_mask` (declared in [floor_map.gdshaderinc](../scenes/levels/floor_map.gdshaderinc), which the x-rays read too): samples inside solid wall pieces (corners as their two arms; visual-only doorway frames stay open) plus every closed door, concealed ones included, so clipping never singles out a secret wall. A `WallClipComponent` (instance [wall_clip_component.tscn](../scenes/components/wall_clip_component.tscn)) renders every mesh under the actor's visual root, gear attached later included, through a `WallClippedMaterial` that rebuilds the authored material on `wall_clipped.gdshader`, and gives each mesh the actor's center as its `wall_clip_anchor` instance parameter. A fragment whose top-down path from that anchor enters a wall sample is discarded ([wall_clip.gdshaderinc](../scenes/levels/wall_clip.gdshaderinc)): a leaning head, headdress or blade may sink into a wall but never shows beyond it, from any camera yaw. The anchor stays on the mesh, so skins, cloak, teleport, the hit flash and the death fade keep clipping; materials the shader cannot reproduce keep their authored material unclipped. The player and the Forsaken Warden carry the component; smaller monsters do not reach through walls and skip the per-mesh cost. Attack swooshes, weapon trails, item auras and monster x-ray silhouettes are separate meshes and are not clipped. While the hero carries a torch or lamp, `PlayerLight` casts a ray from above the player's center to the forward light's usual spot each physics frame. If it hits a wall or door, the light stays on the near side, where the wall shadows the room beyond.

## Reachability

**Every active opening stays accessible, and nothing is placed where actors cannot reach.** After rooms, corridors and walls exist, the floor builds one `WalkabilityGrid` from the real collision (convex outlines, so corner walls keep corners walkable) and flood-fills it from the arrival with every door open, secret doors included. Enemy spawns, chests, traps, loose loot, keys, teleport targets and drop landings only use reachable cells, and enemy spawns and the scheduled fountains also stay out of rooms reached only through undiscovered secret doors; a chest, fountain or keeper is rejected if it would cut off any reachable space. Large actors additionally need reachable cells across their footprint. Inaccessible pockets may exist inside rooms within their type's budget; they simply never receive content. Actual movement follows the [navigation grid](#doors-and-enemy-navigation) built from the same collision.
