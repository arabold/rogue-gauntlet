# Level design

Implemented floor generation: realms and floor plans, room assembly, encounters, loot, traps, lighting, monster navigation and visibility. [Dungeon layout, access and discovery](dungeon-exploration-design.md) owns the intended rules for routes, locks and secrets, [door access](door-access.md) the implemented locks and secrets, and [realm content](realm-content.md) what each realm stages on its floors. The [progression plan](specs/level-progression-prototype-plan.md) keeps the remaining work: guaranteed locations per floor and a visible stair seal.

The [folder guide](../scenes/levels/README.md) maps code and presets, the [room generation harness](../scenes/debug/README.md#room-generation) plays any room type with the game's hero, and the [map generation harness](../scenes/debug/README.md#map-generation) previews whole floors.

## Current generation

Every room, stairs included, is assembled from a `RoomType`; there are no hand-authored rooms. A [`Realm`](../scenes/levels/realms/) is one act of the run: a band of five depths, a theme, its corridor tiles (a `DungeonTileFactory` with a floor material, a wall style and the corridor steps), its room schedule and motifs, weighted floor plans, a boss plan, shops, ambient light and atmosphere. The generator uses the realm covering its depth (`MapGenerator.Realms`, assigned in [level.tscn](../scenes/levels/level.tscn)), and the deepest realm carries on below the last band.

Content is tagged with **themes**, not realms. Room types, wall and floor styles and furnishing steps name the themes they suit in `Realms` (untagged content suits every theme), and a realm assembles only content of its `Theme`. Realms that share a look share a theme, and a room shared by several themes changes with them: the prison is a Garrison cell block in the garrison theme and a slimy brick lock-up in the sewer theme. All realms share the dungeon mesh libraries, each drawing its own subset of the KayKit and [Asset Studio tiles](../asset-studio/tilesets/README.md).

| Depths | Realm | Theme | Gatekeeper on the last floor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–5 | Garrison | garrison | the Forsaken Warden |
| 6–10 | Prison | garrison | the Minotaur |
| 11–15 | Sewer | sewer | the Gelatinous Cube |
| 16–20 | Mines | jungle | none yet |
| 21–25 | Cathedral | garrison | none yet |

**Which plan a floor builds from.** A gatekeeper floor builds from its realm's `BossProfile`. Otherwise a plan the run meets exactly once may claim it (`MapGenerator.UniqueFloorPlans`: the run seed puts the [labyrinth](../scenes/levels/generators/floor_profiles/labyrinth.tres) on one ordinary floor, with no gatekeeper and no shop, between depths 7 and 19). Every other floor draws one of the realm's `FloorPlans` open on it, by weight; each plan has a window of floors. A floor that holds a [trap gauntlet](#trap-gauntlets) draws only plans that can hold one. Every attempt of a floor builds the same plan. `MapGenerator.Profile`, when assigned, pins every floor to one plan for tests, harnesses and previews.

**Room schedule and headlines.** `Realm.Rooms` schedules rooms over windows of the realm's floors (`RealmRoom`), so rooms come in late or retire early. On a realm floor its ordinary scheduled rooms replace the room pool's, with `RealmRoom.Weight` scaling their own weight. Every open floor (no shop, no gatekeeper) gets one **headline**, a special room drawn from the realm's headline candidates whose windows span it; shop and gatekeeper floors are headlined by their keeper or gatekeeper. The run seed alone draws headlines, the most constrained floor first, so every floor of a run agrees, a candidate headlines at most once per realm, and runs rotate which floor gets the library or the chapel. Layouts place the headline before their other rooms and never draw it again; one that does not fit after a few tries is dropped. `RoomDefinition.MaxPerFloor` caps a room's copies. A floor plan's own rooms that the realm does not schedule (the nave, the courtyard, prison cells) stay available to the plans that name them. A realm without a schedule (test fixtures) offers every pool room of its theme, without headlines.

**Motifs.** `Realm.Motifs` (`RealmMotif`) put a theme on some floors, either the whole window or a number of its floors drawn per run. Rooms (`RealmRoom.Motif`) and furnishing steps (`FurnishingStep.Motifs`) that name a motif appear only on floors carrying it, so everything tied to one theme lands together: crypts and bone piles on undead floors, crystal clusters on crystal floors. Wall and floor styles take no motifs yet.

**The pipeline.** Floor-wide choices are made once per floor and shared by every layout attempt: the realm, the special encounters, the floor's share of the schedule, its gauntlet and its plan. Each attempt then lays out rooms and room groups, routes corridors to every doorway, builds walls and corridor decoration, builds floor reachability, resolves the special encounters, places locks and keys, keepers, fountains and loot, builds the navigation grid and spawns the monster packs. Any step that cannot fit retries the layout a bounded number of times; exhaustion reports a generation failure and returns to the menu rather than delivering an invalid floor. Ordinary connectivity and a distant exit do not by themselves guarantee a route or an encounter order; only [room groups](#floor-plans) do.

Generation hands gameplay a `GeneratedFloor` (`Level.Floor`): the layout, rooms, doors, arrival, descent and encounter plan, plus every spatial query gameplay asks (room lookup, footprint clearance, free spawn and landing positions). The generator runs its own placement checks against the same instance and freezes it at handoff. Components read seed, depth, floor, run and exploration from their `Level`, never from the generator or the session. [Randomness and seeds](randomness-and-seeds.md) owns reproducibility and saved differences.

## Floor plans

A `FloorProfile` ([floor_profiles/](../scenes/levels/generators/floor_profiles/)) is a floor's recipe: a room layout (`RoomLayoutStrategy`, in [layouts/](../scenes/levels/generators/layouts/)), a corridor connector (`CorridorConnectorStrategy`, in [connectors/](../scenes/levels/generators/connectors/)), its lock preset (`FloorAccessProfile`, see [door access](door-access.md#generation-and-guaranteed-sources)), map size and room count, doorway overrides, an optional goal that replaces the pool's exits (`GoalOverride`), and loot weighting (`DeadEndLootWeight`, `TrapDensityScale`).

| Plan | Layout and corridors | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| cluster, cluster_large | `PackedRoomLayout`, `AStarCorridorConnector` | Rooms packed and rotated around the entrance, with a geometrically distant exit; the large one is a bigger map with more rooms |
| hallway | `HallwayRoomLayout` | One hallway the length of the floor, the entrance at one end and the exit at the other, side halls branching off it, and rooms lining every hall |
| prison | `HallwayRoomLayout` | The same, lined mostly with one-door cells, cell blocks, guardrooms and storage |
| cathedral, cloister | `CourtyardRoomLayout` | A center room (the aisled nave, or an open courtyard with a pool) ringed by a one-tile walkway; entrance and exit open onto it at opposite ends, side halls leave the middle of each side, and side chapels or monk cells line them |
| mines | `SparseRoomLayout`, `MineCorridorConnector` | A few rooms far apart, joined by tunnels that wander over a seeded rock field and sprout dead-end shafts; the winding tunnels stand in for caves |
| labyrinth | `SparseRoomLayout`, `MazeCorridorConnector` | The entrance, a distant exit and a few rooms in a maze of one-tile corridors with dead ends and a few loops; every room has a door, there are no secret doors, and its locks come in [key chains](door-access.md#generation-and-guaranteed-sources) |
| boss | `LinearRoomLayout` | A spine from the entrance through a few approach rooms to the `boss_exit` group, with optional dead-end side arms |

Hall layouts (`HallRoomLayout`) lay their halls as corridor tiles and line them with the plan's `SideRooms` or, without them, the realm's rooms. Rooms never touch: a corridor or walkway always separates them, which is why the courtyard plans ring their center room with one. Courtyard layouts hold no trap gauntlet.

**Room groups.** A `RoomGroup` ([room_groups/](../scenes/levels/rooms/room_groups/)) is a composite room definition placed as one cluster: a small tree of `RoomGroupMember`s, each a fixed room or a draw from a pool slot (ordinary, entrance, exit, gauntlet), joined to an earlier member across a one-tile sealed bridge. A member marked `MustTraverse` cannot be bypassed: generation checks the finished floor and retries a layout where the descent is reachable around it. `ReserveAccess` keeps generated locks off a member, and members can forbid secret doors or doorways to the outside. `boss_exit` puts the exit room behind the gatekeeper's boss room, which is assembled with the gatekeeper's minimum clearance; `gauntlet_exit` puts it behind a trap gauntlet. On the boss plan, corridors join exactly the doorways the layout paired and separate branches never fuse, so the spine stays a spine.

**Corridor edges and corridor loot.** The map records walls between neighbouring corridor tiles, so maze passages and parallel corridors can run side by side; one query (`MapData.HasWallToward`) decides walls, corridor decoration, loot spots and sight. A wall between two walkable tiles uses an opaque, two-sided piece, never a window, gate or one-sided accent. Keys, loose items and wall-backed chests may lie in corridors as well as rooms, weighted toward dead ends by the plan's `DeadEndLootWeight`; fountains stay in rooms.

## Encounters

<a id="first-realm-encounter-prototype"></a>

Each realm's encounters are a `RealmEncounterFactory` in [encounters/](../scenes/levels/encounters/): the [Garrison](../scenes/levels/encounters/sunken_garrison.tres), [Prison](../scenes/levels/encounters/prison_encounters.tres), [Sewer](../scenes/levels/encounters/sewer_encounters.tres) and [Mines](../scenes/levels/encounters/mines_encounters.tres) chain through their `Fallback`. The Cathedral has none yet: below the Mines the development fallback (`dungeon_mob_factory.tres`, skeletons and rats) fills the floors. [Realm content](realm-content.md) lists each realm's cast by floor and [monster definitions](monster-catalog/README.md) supply fixed strength.

Entries are weighted, depth-bounded **packs**: monsters that spawn together. A floor spends a budget that the level's [threat profile](../scenes/levels/encounters/threat_profile.tres) gives its depth; a pack costs its members' threat-level costs times `(N + 1) / 2`, because members fighting together outlast being fought one at a time (see the [balance model](balance-model.md#floors-and-gatekeepers)). The level owns the profile, which also sets the XP monsters pay, and generation passes it to the factory, so realm encounter resources never name it. A gatekeeper floor spends only a share of its budget on packs. A monster's `MaximumPerFloor` caps how many of it one floor's packs hold, so a cheap monster cannot fill the end of every budget. Each pack's first member takes a spawn point spaced from every other pack, the rest stand close to it in the same room, and a member without space is dropped.

Special encounters are planned before anything is placed (`EncounterPlan`). Each realm's second floor turns two pack slots into **nests**: [bone nests](../scenes/levels/encounters/bone_nest.tscn) in the Garrison, [bat roosts](../scenes/levels/encounters/bat_roost.tscn) in the Prison and the Mines, [slime vats](../scenes/levels/encounters/slime_vat.tscn) in the Sewer. Nests activate in revealed nearby space, limit simultaneous and total summons, and never refill killed slots; destroying one stops further summons while released minions stay. The last floor of a realm with a gatekeeper (`HasGatekeeper`) holds it. From the Prison on, each placed chest may be a chest mimic instead (`MimicChance`), which takes the chest's persistent ID. Nests and gatekeepers replace whole packs, so budgets alone do not measure a floor's difficulty.

A room may provide a `GatekeeperSpawnMarker` to control the gatekeeper's position and facing after rotation; author one anchor and keep its physical clearance. Otherwise generation picks the roomiest supported location nearest the descent: outside the arrival's room and, on the boss plan, in the boss room or the exit room behind it. The site is decided once, before locks and loot: locks never seal its room, loot keeps clear of its footprint, and the gatekeeper takes the first encounter slot there. A layout without a site is retried, and failure returns safely instead of loading an invalid floor. Tall enemies stay hidden until their tile is discovered.

The descent stays sealed until the gatekeeper dies: a label over the down-stairs states the objective, and both the stairs and session travel refuse to descend (`Level.CanDescend`). A visible barrier is [still planned](specs/level-progression-prototype-plan.md). Cleared enemies, destroyed nests, killed summon slots and gatekeeper defeats persist across revisits; living actors reset to their generated state, without partial health, position or summon timers. See [monster authoring](monster-authoring-design.md) for tactics and the [roadmap](development-roadmap.md#first-realm-acceptance) for playtest acceptance.

## Fountains

The generator's `Fountains` profile ([fountain_profile.tres](../scenes/props/fountains/fountain_profile.tres)) schedules health and mana fountains across the whole run. Each `FountainKind` draws its few fountains from the run seed alone, so every floor agrees without shared state: the profile's depth range splits into equal bands, one fountain per band, and kinds avoid sharing a floor. A floor's fountain uses that kind's stream to pick one of its designs and a free-standing room tile, preferring tiles walkable on every side and skipping connector-adjacent ones. Like chests, a fountain is a solid obstacle that must stand on reachable floor, keep door swings clear and leave no inaccessible pocket; a floor that cannot fit one logs it and the run has one fewer.

`Fountain` grants its `FountainBlessing` strategy (`RestoreHealthBlessing`, `RestoreManaBlessing`) once, through the ordinary timed interaction; a drinker whose pool is full sees a blocked prompt. Drinking hides the art's `Liquid` node, plays the [pool effects](world-effects.md#fountain-effects) and the drinker's use-item gesture (an interaction offer can name an animation action). The fountain's `depth:N:fountain:<kind>` id joins the run's cleared entities, so a revisited or loaded floor rebuilds it drained.

## Trap gauntlets

A run meets a few [trap gauntlets](combat-system.md#trap-hazards), never the same room twice, each guarding a floor's descent. Gauntlets are set pieces on the route, never secret: crossing them is the challenge. The generator's `Gauntlets` profile ([gauntlet_profile.tres](../scenes/levels/generators/gauntlet_profile.tres), `GauntletProfile`) deals them from the run seed alone: it spreads the chosen floors over the ordinary floors (no gatekeeper, shop or labyrinth) whose realm has a plan that can hold the approach, and deals each the first gauntlet room left in a shuffled deck that suits its theme. A pinned `Profile` builds every floor without one.

On its floor the descent builds from the approach group ([gauntlet_exit.tres](../scenes/levels/rooms/room_groups/gauntlet_exit.tres)) instead of a plain exit: the gauntlet, then the exit room behind it, joined at the gauntlet's far doorway. The gauntlet is `MustTraverse`, and neither room takes secret doors or generated locks. A floor whose layouts keep finding no room for the approach goes without its gauntlet. Gauntlet rooms hold nothing to fight or pick up: their tiles count as occupied, so no monster, loot, key, chest, fountain, keeper or floor trap lands there, and teleports and drops land outside.

The gauntlet rooms (`rooms/room_types/gauntlets/`: the arrow gallery's dart slits, the gargoyle crypt's fire-spitting heads and the pendulum hall's blades) suit the garrison, sewer and jungle themes in each theme's walls and floors. The pool keeps them in its own `Gauntlets` slot, which only the approach group draws. Their first step, a hazard row step (`CrossFireStep` or `PendulumRowsStep`, see the [authoring guide](../scenes/levels/generators/assembly/README.md#add-a-room-type)), lays rows across the door-to-door spine:

- Each row spans the room's full width, so it cannot be walked around, and reserves its band, so furniture never blocks a lane or a swing. Hazards themselves do not block, so walkability and navigation are unchanged.
- Rows stand at least one tile apart, so the hero can stop between them, and share one `TrapGauntlet` and its beat. Patterns stagger rows by fractions of the beat, never by a walking speed.
- Rows stop at the first that cannot be laid, so a gauntlet never has a gap twice its spacing; too few rows retry the room. A spout row whose wall has no plain stretch there hangs its spout on the opposite wall; a blade narrows its swing to its space, short of the wall or halfway to its neighbour.
- One gauntlet mixes at most two hazard kinds, and monsters are immune to them.

## Triggers

A trigger links a **source** that is on or off to **receivers** ([scenes/props/triggers/](../scenes/props/triggers/)). A source reports each change, with the actor that caused it, through its `TriggerOutputComponent`, which carries it to its `Targets` in one of three modes (`TriggerMode` strategies): **Pulse** pulses the receivers each time the source turns on, **Hold** switches them on and off with the source, and **Latch** switches them on the first time and never off. Receivers implement `ITriggerable` (a one-time pulse, a lasting on and off); a target that is not one is a configuration warning. Neither side knows the other's type.

The one source today is the `PressurePlate`: on while something its trigger's collision mask accepts stands on it (only the hero, for traps). A body passing over without touching down, such as a dodging hero (`IFloorContact`), does not press it. A concealed plate is found by proximity like a secret door, or revealed when pressed. The receivers are the spike trap's `SpikeBurst` (the hidden floor trap is a concealed plate pulsing its own spikes) and the `WallLauncher` (one shot per pulse, a shot every interval while on); their combat is in [combat](combat-system.md#trap-hazards) and their placement under [loot](#loot). Plate discovery is saved by plate ID. Switches, puzzle conditions, items weighing down plates, doors as receivers and saved latches are [proposed](specs/triggers-plan.md).

## Shops

A realm's `Shops` stand keepers on the realm floors their placements name, as solid props placed after locks and keys and before loot. A layout with no spot for a keeper is retried. [Shops](shops.md#placement) owns the placement rules.

## Loot

Most of a floor's loot lies waiting to be explored, as in Rogue and Shattered Pixel Dungeon, rather than dropping from kills. `MapGenerator.Loot.cs` places it after the keys, keepers and fountains, every spot spaced from the others and clear of the arrival and the stair landings:

- **Reward chests.** Every room behind a generated lock (except a locked exit) holds a gold chest ([chest_gold.tscn](../scenes/props/chest_gold.tscn)), and every room reached only through secret doors a secret chest ([chest_secret.tscn](../scenes/props/chest_secret.tscn)), each on a better table. Neither is ever a mimic. A room too furnished for a chest gets the chest's loot on its floor instead.
- **Chests:** a few per floor, backed against walls in rooms and in corridor dead ends and corners, never in a one-tile bottleneck; from the Prison on, each may be a mimic.
- **Loose items and gold piles:** a few spots each, rolled from [loose_items.tres](../scenes/items/loot/drops/loose_items.tres) and [gold_piles.tres](../scenes/items/loot/drops/gold_piles.tres).
- **Traps:** a few hidden trap slots (`MinTraps..MaxTraps`, scaled by the plan's `TrapDensityScale`). Each is a spike plate on an open room or corridor tile, chokepoints included, or, at `TriggeredTrapChance`, a plate trap: a concealed plate and a wall launcher across from it, picked by weight among the `TriggeredTraps` profiles ([`scenes/props/traps/triggered/`](../scenes/props/traps/triggered/)) whose first depth the floor has reached: darts from depth 2, fireballs from the Prison, held darts from the Sewer. The lane runs from a plain stretch of room wall, where the launcher hangs as rooms hang wall decor (clear of decor already there and of doors), to the plate a few metres out in the same room. The plate keeps clear of doorways, grates and drains and leaves room to walk past, and no solid stands in the shot's way by the floor's real collision outlines. A slot without a fitting lane keeps the spike plate.

Each spot is its own seeded reward source. [Stats and itemization](stats-and-itemization.md#loot-eligibility) describes the tables.

## Room geometry and stairs

Every room is a `Room` with floor, wall and decoration GridMaps built by its room type. GridMap cells are one world unit with centering disabled; logical map tiles are **4×4 units centered on their anchor**, whatever the size of the floor meshes. The room owns its doorways completely: assembly hands the room its tile map as data (footprint tiles as floor, stairwell and pool pits as chasms, each doorway as a connector facing outward) and fills every doorway at once with its wall style's door, its opening frame or nothing (an open walkway); every other edge is walled. Nothing re-derives the map from geometry or adds, swaps or removes a doorway's door later: the room hands each door to the floor with the doorway it fills, and a doorway the corridors cannot reach fails the layout. The generator rotates complete rooms in quarter-turns: GridMaps, props and doors as geometry, the tile map as data.

Room and floor walls come from one boundary fitter. It resolves edge junctions with the wall family's corner, T and crossing pieces and keeps a chosen straight variant, accent or pillar only where its measured footprint fits; otherwise the style's plain straight piece stands. Door frames and secret disguises reserve their 4×1 footprint, and short pieces join perpendicular walls at the frame face. Frames add no permanent collision, so an open door leaves the whole doorway clear. Assembly rejects a candidate whose wall regions overlap, and a layout whose corridor walls cannot fit around a room's divider is retried. Wall assets must conform to their declared footprints and seams ([authoring](../scenes/levels/generators/assembly/README.md#add-a-floor-or-wall-style)).

The stairs are scenes ([up](../scenes/levels/stairs/stairs_up.tscn), [down](../scenes/levels/stairs/stairs_down.tscn)) placed by the entrance and exit room types' `StairsStep`: the staircase with its collision, the arrival `PlayerSpawnPoint` and the `LevelTransitionTrigger`. Up-stairs stand flush against the wall behind their top. Down-stairs sink into a two-tile stairwell cut into open floor, lined by stone walls one floor below with their tops flush with the floor; the room maps the stairwell as an enclosed chasm. Either way the landing at the walk-on end is claimed for the rest of the assembly: no floor prop covers it and every doorway must keep reaching it. Generated loot, keys and traps keep clear of every landing, and floor reachability floods from the arrival's landing.

Corridor floor materials and wall styles validate before room generation; missing assignments or invalid tiles report a generation failure.

## Room assembly

Every room is assembled with its content by a `RoomType` ([room types](../scenes/levels/rooms/room_types/)): one strategy, or a weighted palette of strategies, per axis. `RoomAssembler` runs them in a fixed order (shape, doorways, walls, circulation, columns, furnishing steps, floor, props), each axis on its own derived stream, so changing a floor style or toggling columns never moves furniture. Rooms are finished before floor layout and rotate as a whole. A required step that cannot fit retries with a new shape (`Attempts`); a type that never fits throws instead of delivering a different room, naming the step and the last placement it refused. Ordinary room types range from compact to large while cells, closets and plan rooms stay small; each recipe authors its sizes and minimum floor area. The [authoring guide](../scenes/levels/generators/assembly/README.md) covers adding props, groups, styles, steps and room types.

| Axis | Strategies |
| --- | --- |
| Shape and size | `RectangleShape` (optional odd width), `LShape`, `TShape`, `UShape`, `CrossShape`; width across, length along the room axis (+Z) |
| Doorways | `MinDoorways`/`MaxDoorways`, `DoorwayPlacement` (any, long-axis ends, long sides, or a single end that leaves the far wall for a focal point), `SecretDoorChance` per doorway (a secret always creates a door) with its `SecretDetection`, `DoorChance` for the rest, then `FrameChance` for the wall style's opening frame; the rest are open walkways |
| Circulation | Doorway approaches or a door-to-door spine kept free of furniture |
| Walls | `WallStyle`: weighted straight pieces and accents, an optional pillar, the doorway `DoorScene` and `Opening`, and its family's structural pieces (KayKit's by default; cave walls bring their own) |
| Floor | `UniformFloor`, `BorderedFloor`, `SplitFloor` over `FloorMaterial`s; a split runs one straight or diagonal boundary across the room, never scattered patches; `FloorInlay`s set drains, gutters and channels into the tiling; an optional `FloorRug` lays a runner or a bordered rug without collision |
| Columns | `AisleColumns`, `PerimeterColumns`, `CenterColumns`, `CornerColumns`, behind `ColumnChance`; symmetric groups place all-or-nothing |
| Furnishing | `AlongWallsStep`, `CornersStep`, `RowsStep`, `CenterStep`, `ScatterStep`, `WallDecorStep`, `CellsStep` (barred cells with gates), `FencedAreaStep` (bays, walkways, dividers), `ArcadeStep` (rows of arches), `PoolStep` (water sunk into the floor), the hazard row steps of gauntlet rooms, `StairsStep` and `ChoiceStep` (one weighted variant); `MinimumCount` makes a step required, `Chance` occasional, and `Uniform` keeps one content choice per room |

Steps place `Placeable`s. A `Prop` is one object: mesh-library items (cosmetic variants) or scenes for props that need nodes, measured from the model. Its front faces local +Z; back-to-wall props stand with -Z on the face of the wall pieces behind them, and wall props hang on the mounting surface at `MountHeight`, measured from the wall's band above 0.7 m so plinths and rubble never push decor off the wall. Along the wall, decor hangs only inside the **mount spans** the wall models declare (exported by the Asset Studio, read by `WallFootprints.MeasureEdge`): cave walls declare the bays between their timber posts, so decor centers on each bay and never crosses a post; walls that declare none take decor along their whole length. Once the floor's corridor walls stand, `MapGenerator` takes down every room wall prop a corridor junction left outside its wall's mount spans. A `PropGroup` is an anchor plus optional members at offsets (a bed with nightstand and chest); `SeatedTable` spaces one kind of seat along a measured table. Every piece is validated on its own, so a member that does not fit is dropped, and pieces may rest on others. Weighted picks are balanced over the room, and loose props carry a small seeded turn and shift so they sit naturally off the packing grid.

`RoomBlueprint` checks every piece for floor support, wall and prop overlap (with each prop's `Gap`) and circulation, and keeps all doorways mutually reachable on a 0.5-unit `WalkabilityGrid` sized for the largest ordinary walking body (`RoomAssembler.ActorRadius`, the skeletons'; the player's capsule is slimmer). Inaccessible pockets behind furniture are allowed up to the type's `MaxPocketShare` of standable floor. Interactive props reserve the space in front of them. Walkability tests a quarter-cell lattice of sample points against the full actor radius, so a gap is open exactly when the actor fits through it, wherever the grid falls. Assembled props are plain nodes under the room's `Props` with static collision on the prop layer, so they block actors and navigation but not exploration sight. Prison gates are ordinary doors with [interior identities](door-access.md#interior-cell-gates).

Fitted structure can mark room-local floor patches for the later cosmetic pass: `CellsStep.CellFloors` chooses one dirt for all of a prison's cell rows, independent of its aisle, on its own cosmetic stream, without changing furniture or walkability.

Arcades divide rooms without closing them off. `ArcadeStep` stands rows of arches from wall to wall, cutting a sewer vault into vaulted bays or lining a chapel's or cistern hall's sides; the jambs and closing piers block bodies and projectiles, so a fight moves through the openings, and every bay stays reachable.

Water is impassable to walkers. `PoolStep` cuts whole tiles out of the floor and fills them with pool pieces: quay walls around the perimeter, open water between. Scattered pools keep a dry border, cistern halls a centered pool with dry routes around it, and drain tunnels a dry walkway with water to one or both side walls; every layout keeps the doorways connected. The room maps water as chasm tiles, so it reveals with the room but hosts no walking actor, loot or trap; actor-only barriers on the `walking_terrain` physics layer block entry while projectiles and sight pass. Walkable inlays use grates rather than open water. Every water surface is drawn by the [water shader](../scenes/levels/dungeon/water/water.gdshader).

### Corridor styling and decoration

Each realm's `DungeonTileFactory` pairs a `FloorMaterial` and `WallStyle` with an ordered list of `CorridorStep` strategies (`CorridorPassagewayStep`, `CorridorThresholdStep`, `CorridorCornerStep`, `CorridorHallwayStep`, and in the Mines `CorridorRailStep` and `CorridorDeadEndStep`). The floor's boundary pass uses the corridor wall style's structural pieces; compatible room and corridor wall variants survive it where their footprints fit, and straight and half pieces face inward. `CorridorBlueprint` then measures decoratable straight wall runs (excluding accents, corners, doorways and concealed secret-door walls), doorway thresholds, corners, T-junctions, dead ends and straight one-tile spans, runs each step on its own derived stream and emits the props under `CorridorProps`.

Mine rails lay connected runs of straight, curved and buffer-stop pieces, from a dead end back up its shaft or along a straight span. A run is checked tile by tile before any piece goes down and laid up to the first piece that no longer fits, so tracks never have gaps; buffer stops end runs only at dead ends, where a cart may park. Dead-end props (carts, ore piles) then fill the shafts the rails left free.

## Lighting and atmosphere

Every playing world shares `world_lighting.tscn` and `game_environment.tres`: neutral ambient fill, a subdued reflection sky (reflections only, never the background), warm local fixtures, contact occlusion and restrained height haze. A realm whose surfaces read darker scales and tints that fill on its floors (`Realm.AmbientScale`, `Realm.AmbientTint`). Rendering quality settings live under **Project Settings → Rendering** and in `game_environment.tres`; revisit the fog length when changing camera distance, pitch or zoom.

**Darkness.** A dark floor disables ambient light, sky reflections, haze and fixture flames; fog-of-war reveal is independent of lighting. Today darkness is only the debug menu's **Dark level** switch; runs do not generate dark floors. The hero's `PlayerLight` uses the `Normal` profile on ordinary floors and `Lit` on dark ones while a torch or lamp is equipped, and a soft, shadowless `Dark` fill without one (profiles beside `player_light.tscn`); the [darkness screen effect](screen-effects.md) fades that view to black around the hero. While the hero carries a light, a ray from above the player keeps the forward light on the near side of walls and doors. Rooms and corridor palettes expose `FixtureLitChance`: each fixture's initial state is chosen on a separate cosmetic seed, so lighting never changes placement.

**Surface finishes.** `DungeonSurfaceFinish` applies the `SurfaceFinishProfile` resources under `scenes/levels/lighting/surface_finishes/` to private copies of the generator's mesh libraries before generation: the first matching item-name prefix selects a cloth, damp stone, wood or dry stone response. Only roughness and specular change; geometry, IDs, collision, authored metal, deliberate gloss and custom shaders are preserved.

**Focus blur.** `focus_blur_effect.tscn` on the `ScreenEffects` layer keeps a central ellipse sharp and blurs toward the edges, in viewport fractions, so zooming out protects more world space. **FocusBlur → Intensity** scales or disables it; the shader parameters set the ellipse and falloff. Camera depth of field stays off.

**Atmosphere.** Each `Realm.Atmosphere` selects an `AtmosphereProfile` (`scenes/levels/lighting/atmosphere_profiles/`) whose `Effects` are scenes rooted in an `AtmosphereEffect` (`atmosphere_effects/`): haze (`AtmosphereHaze`), particle layers such as dust, motes and falling dust (`AtmosphereParticles`), dripping water (`drips.tscn`), persistent marks (`AtmosphereSurfaceMarks`: wet patches, scuffs along passages, blood splatters and drag marks limited to cells, cell blocks and interrogation rooms) and dust rolling down walls in view (`AtmosphereWallDust`). `DungeonAtmosphere` owns their floor lifetime and the shared darkness gate; the floor context supplies cached playable-surface masks, bounds and emission fields. All atmosphere randomness is cosmetic and independent of effect order, so composition changes neither layout nor saved state. Darkness suspends effects unless their `ActiveInDarkness` allows them. A new atmosphere is a profile choosing effect scenes; a new effect implements the small `AtmosphereEffect` preparation, activation and release contract and needs no slot in the controller, level or realm. Effects and haze keep their colors, densities and motion in their exported and native material settings, use private runtime materials, and read the shared exploration coverage, so mist never exposes hidden rooms.

**Surface marks.** Weathering uses native `SurfaceMark` decals with shared `SurfaceMarkProfile` resources (`scenes/effects/surface_marks/`): tint, blending, optional roughness, and authored masks or procedural edges. Wet marks darken and gloss surfaces; scuffs and dried blood use color alone. Fixtures author their own soot (torches and hearths on the wall behind them, braziers on the floor). Actors leave marks through a `TrailMarksComponent`, which drops its mark every `Spacing` metres while its actor moves over revealed floor and recycles a fixed ring of marks (the acid blob's slime trail). To add a stain, duplicate a profile and mark scene and attach it to a prop or an atmosphere mark layer.

**Reflections.** The level owns one `LocalReflectionProbe`, focused on the player by `Main`, which recaptures nearby geometry in once-update mode after enough travel and a cooldown. Box projection gives wet decals and glossy materials approximate room reflections; capture excludes character and effect layers and switches off in darkness.

**Debug → Visual effects** toggles fog, ambient occlusion, focus blur, surface marks, drips, atmospheric dust, wind and reflections per world (`WorldLighting.DisabledEffects`), without touching authored resources; each `AtmosphereEffect.Category` opts its scene into a switch. For performance comparisons, keep camera, floor and resolution fixed and compare frame milliseconds, since a frame cap hides gains above it.

Selected props opt into [ambient motion](ambient-motion.md) through `Prop.Motion`; generated levels and the room harness supply a world-local airflow after placement.

### Light flicker

Every flickering light shares one Inspector-tunable `FlickerProfile` that turns noise into a brightness multiplier. Presets live in `scenes/effects/flicker_profiles/`: `torch` for wall torches and candles, `dim` for the player's dim light, `flame` for fire spells, `electric` for lightning strobes and `charged` for the storm trident's glow. Each light starts at its own random offset, so the torches of one floor never flicker in step. Standalone lights are `FlickeringLight` nodes, which flicker around their `Energy` only while visible; lights a spell effect already drives apply the flicker through `EffectLight`. Torch flames also carry sparse dust motes and faint smoke that share their fixture's darkness and discovery gates.

## Doors and enemy navigation

Monsters navigate on the floor's `ClearanceGrid`, not a navigation mesh. Once every solid is placed, generation measures the distance to the nearest solid on the same quarter-cell lattice as walkability, from the same tiles and collision outlines (inside the actor height band), and keeps each 0.5-unit cell's roomiest sample. A cell is traversable for a body radius when that clearance covers the whole radius, so one grid serves every walker: each goes only where its body capsule fits, steering toward the roomiest point of each cell. Every door, interior cell gates included, is a gate over its shut leaf's footprint; its frame and its open leaf are ordinary solids. Closed doors block physics, exploration sight and navigation; barred cell gates block traversal but let exploration into the cell. Opening or closing a door changes routes on the next query.

Flyers navigate a second grid, `GeneratedFloor.AirClearance`, built from the same walls, void, gates and tall solids: pits and water count as floor, and solids no taller than `ClearanceGrid.AirClearanceHeight` and water's walking barriers do not block, so a flyer crosses pools and low furniture but never walls, shut doors or tall props. A monster picks its grid with `NavigationComponent.Layer`; the level and the debug arena serve grids by layer through `INavigableWorld.NavigationFor`. A drop with no free spot in throwing range, such as loot from a bat killed over water, lands on the nearest free floor in sight.

The level's `FloorNavigation` computes shared Dijkstra fields on the grid. A chase field per target and body size leads every chaser of that size toward its target, computed only as far out as its farthest chaser needs and refreshed when stale or when a door changes, so a crowd costs little more than one monster. Cells tighter than the body radius plus a `NavigationProfile.PreferredMargin` cost more, so routes keep to the middle of corridors and doorways while open rooms still get direct lines. A flee field derived from it leads away from a threat and out of dead ends; roaming, searching and ring points use bounded A* routes with the same costs. `NavigationComponent` aims at the farthest cell down the field it can walk to in a straight line, heads straight for a nearby target once the line is clear, steers apart from other monsters and side-steps toward open space before reporting a stall; a monster makes progress only while its remaining route shrinks. A monster whose `EnemyBehaviorProfile.StayInHomeRoom` is set (the gatekeepers, the stone golem, the chest mimic) moves anywhere in its spawn room but holds at its edge. Fields, routes and reachability are plain C#, tested headless; the debug menu's Navigation overlay draws the grid. [Monster authoring](monster-authoring-design.md#awareness-and-pursuit) owns detection and pursuit.

Every walkable floor tile collides as one flat box at a shared height; surface detail and the slightly higher dirt and rubble tiles are visual only, so bodies never snag on seams. On stairs, a body pushing into a step with open space one step up is lifted over it.

Known limitations:

- **One plane:** the grids are 2D, so stairs are not monster terrain, and nothing swims.
- **Static apart from doors:** only doors change the grids at runtime; a destroyed nest keeps its cells blocked until the floor is rebuilt.
- **Low obstacles:** anything rising into the actor height band blocks every walker, so curbs and pool rims stop rats as well.
- **Wide gatekeepers:** a body wider than an open door's clear width passes only doorless openings, so releasing a gatekeeper from its room needs more than a data change.
- **Doors:** monsters never open doors; a closed door that cuts off the route turns a chase into a search.

## Map occlusion and fog of war

Permanent black caps hide void under the rotating isometric camera. Exploration uses bounded omnidirectional line of sight from the player, independent of room entry, facing, lighting and camera zoom. Walls, unsanctioned room/corridor edges and closed door thresholds block sight; open doorways reveal only their visible wedge, and exact diagonal corner crossings need both routes open. Furnishings do not block discovery. Editor previews expose generated space.

Discovery records an eight-by-eight sample grid per tile. Seen samples stay explored permanently, across travel and save/load; unseen parts of a partly explored tile stay covered. A depth-based screen shader maps rendered surfaces back to this world-aligned history, feathers the boundary inward and fades newly discovered space in. There is no dimmed remembered-space state.

Exploration belongs to the level's `FloorDiscovery` node ([floor_discovery.tscn](../scenes/levels/floor_discovery.tscn), wired to `Level.Discovery`), whose `SightRadius`, `FadeSeconds` and `FeatherWidth` are exported. Each floor owns its exploration texture and publishes it with the map bounds and fade clock as `fog_*` global shader uniforms ([fog_of_war.gdshaderinc](../scenes/levels/fog_of_war.gdshaderinc)), so the fog and the silhouettes above it share one coverage test. Sight updates at a bounded rate while moving and refreshes after door changes; a closed door seals its threshold because sight reads the door itself.

Content gates stay tile-based: discovering any sample on a tile enables its loot, effects and eligible x-ray silhouettes, and `ExplorationGateComponent` hides torch visuals, particles and lights until then. Discovered loot does not disappear when the player looks away. The debug fog reveal fills the history, so gated loot, traps and effects appear with it.

Closed-door x-ray silhouettes appear only while the door is hidden (occluded, or in view on still-unexplored space), nearby and adjacent to revealed space. Unopened chests use a golden occlusion silhouette on discovered tiles, behind the loot X-ray toggle. Door, chest, monster and loot silhouettes draw above the fog, but their discovery gates keep them from revealing unexplored space. None draws over an actor in view: monsters and loot mark their visible pixels in a stencil mask, the hero does the same through its `XrayOccluderComponent`, and every x-ray skips those pixels. A hidden pixel shows only when what hides it is a wall, a closed door or fogged ground ([xray_occlusion.gdshaderinc](../scenes/levels/xray_occlusion.gdshaderinc)), reconstructed from the depth buffer and checked against `floor_wall_mask` and the fog, so new effects and props never need their own x-ray exception. Without a loaded floor (harnesses and previews) every occluder counts. [Door access](door-access.md) adds concealed entrances, locks and key markers: a concealed door exposes no silhouette, prompt or marker. `Level.CaptureExploration` saves doors, plates and samples together.

Actors' visuals stop at walls. `FloorDiscovery` also publishes the floor's `WallMask` as `floor_wall_mask` ([floor_map.gdshaderinc](../scenes/levels/floor_map.gdshaderinc)): samples inside solid wall pieces plus every closed door, concealed ones included, so clipping never singles out a secret wall. A `WallClipComponent` ([wall_clip_component.tscn](../scenes/components/wall_clip_component.tscn)) renders every mesh under the actor's visual root, later gear included, through a `WallClippedMaterial` on `wall_clipped.gdshader`, anchored at the actor's center; a fragment whose top-down path from that anchor enters a wall sample is discarded ([wall_clip.gdshaderinc](../scenes/levels/wall_clip.gdshaderinc)), from any camera yaw. Skins, cloak, teleport, the hit flash and the death fade keep clipping. The player and the Forsaken Warden carry the component; smaller monsters do not reach through walls and skip its cost. Attack swooshes, weapon trails, item auras and x-ray silhouettes are not clipped.

## Reachability

**Every active opening stays accessible, and nothing is placed where actors cannot reach.** After rooms, corridors and walls exist, the floor builds one `WalkabilityGrid` from the real collision (convex outlines, so corner walls keep corners walkable) and flood-fills it from the arrival with every door open, secret doors included. Enemy spawns, chests, traps, loose loot, keys, teleport targets and drop landings use only reachable cells; enemy spawns and fountains also stay out of rooms reached only through undiscovered secret doors, and nothing lands in a gauntlet room. A chest, fountain or keeper is rejected if it would cut off any reachable space, and large actors need reachable cells across their footprint. Inaccessible pockets may exist inside rooms within their type's budget; they never receive content. Actual movement follows the [navigation grid](#doors-and-enemy-navigation) built from the same collision.
