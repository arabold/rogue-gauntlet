# Level design

Implemented generation, room authoring, encounters and visibility. [Dungeon layout, access and discovery](dungeon-exploration-design.md) owns intended realm pools, recipes, locks and secrets; [door access](door-access.md) describes implemented locks/secrets and the [progression plan](specs/level-progression-prototype-plan.md) retains future realm recipes and seals.

See the [folder guide](../scenes/levels/README.md) for code and preset locations, and the [room generation harness](../scenes/debug/README.md#room-generation) for single-room and full-floor previews.

## Current generation

Every room is assembled; there are no hand-authored rooms. All rooms come from one [room pool](../scenes/levels/rooms/room_pool.tres). A [`Realm`](../scenes/levels/realms/) names a band of depths and its corridor tiles (a floor material and a wall style, so corridors match its rooms); the generator uses the realm covering its depth, and the deepest realm carries on below the last band. Room types, wall and floor styles and furnishing steps carry `Realms` tags: a realm assembles only the room types tagged for it, each with the styles and steps tagged for it (untagged styles and steps suit every realm). A room shared by several realms therefore changes with them: the prison is a Garrison prison or a Sewer lock-up with slimy walls and brick floors. All realms share the dungeon mesh libraries; each draws a different subset of the KayKit and [Asset Studio tiles](../asset-studio/tilesets/README.md) in them.

| Depths | Realm | Its own rooms | Shared rooms | Corridors |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–3 | Garrison | hall, mess hall, barracks, library, alchemist, passage, fenced walkway, chapel, kitchen, guardroom, crypt, root cellar | prison, storage, partitioned storage, ruins, entrance, exit | KayKit dirt, stone/arched/gated walls, passageways, torches & heraldic decor |
| 4–6 | Sewer | cistern hall, drain tunnel, junction, rat warren | prison, storage, partitioned storage, ruins, entrance, exit | studio bricks, slimy/gated walls, stone/scaffold passageways, torches & shackles |
| 7–9 | Jungle | temple hall, root passage, cavern, timbered mine, ruined shrine | ruins, entrance, exit | studio earth, mossy/overgrown/scaffold walls, cave/timber passageways, torches & trophies |

The entrance and exit carry the stairs. Encounters do not follow realms: the [encounter prototype](#first-realm-encounter-prototype) covers depths 1–3 and later depths use its development fallback. Per-depth room roles and required routes remain proposed.

Ordinary room recipes mix compact and medium footprints, with larger halls, chapels and cell blocks reserved for their spatial needs. Sizes and minimum floor areas are authored per recipe, and furnishing strategies keep their required identity while packing optional content into the available space. Logical tiles are 4×4 world units; the room harness reports both the bounding dimensions and footprint area for non-rectangular shapes.

Generation packs and rotates rooms around an entrance, prefers a geometrically distant exit, connects every doorway, walls corridors, records room entrances, builds floor reachability, decides special encounters, assigns validated lock/key pairs, then places other loot and builds the navigation grid. Ordinary encounters use the completed geometry. Ordinary connectivity and a distant exit do not guarantee a mandatory route or encounter order.

Generation hands gameplay a `GeneratedFloor` (`Level.Floor`): the layout, rooms, doors, arrival, descent and encounter plan, plus every spatial query gameplay asks (room lookup, footprint clearance, free spawn and landing positions). The generator runs its own placement checks against the same instance, and freezes it at handoff. Components read seed, depth, floor, run and exploration from their `Level`, never from the generator or the session.

Generation settings and factories are assigned in [level.tscn](../scenes/levels/level.tscn). [Randomness and seeds](randomness-and-seeds.md) owns reproducibility and saved differences.

## First-realm encounter prototype

The first three depths use the [Sunken Garrison encounter resource](../scenes/levels/encounters/sunken_garrison.tres). Weighted, depth-bounded entries spend a floor budget that the [threat profile](../scenes/levels/encounters/threat_profile.tres) gives each depth; each monster costs what its threat level is worth there (see the [balance model](specs/balance-model.md#cost-threat-level-and-xp)). Clearance may reduce the number actually placed. The factory's encounter plan substitutes nests and the gatekeeper for ordinary slots, so budgets alone do not measure final floor difficulty. [Monster definitions](monster-catalog/README.md) supply fixed strength. Other depths use an explicit development fallback, not finished realm content.

- Depth 1 introduces ordinary skeleton encounters.
- Depth 2 adds rats and finite nests. Nests activate in revealed nearby space, limit simultaneous summons and total slots, and never refill killed slots. Destroying a nest collapses bound survivors without XP or loot.
- Depth 3 adds the Forsaken Warden. Defeat unlocks downward travel through an objective check. Visible seals, boss phases and guaranteed reward-room topology are not implemented.

A room may provide a `GatekeeperSpawnMarker` to control boss position and facing after rotation. Author one intended anchor and ensure its physical clearance. Otherwise generation picks the roomiest supported location nearest the descent, and retries a bounded number of deterministic layouts; failure returns safely instead of loading an invalid floor. The site is decided once, before locks and loot: locks never seal its room, loot keeps clear of its minimum footprint, and the gatekeeper takes the first encounter slot there. Large-actor movement retains its clearance constraints, and tall enemies stay hidden until their tile is discovered.

Cleared enemies, destroyed nests, killed summon slots and Warden defeat persist across revisits. Living actors reset to their generated initial state; partial health, position and summon timers are not saved. See [monster authoring](monster-authoring-design.md) for tactics and the [roadmap](development-roadmap.md#first-three-floor-acceptance) for playtest acceptance.

## Fountains

The generator's `Fountains` profile ([fountain_profile.tres](../scenes/props/fountains/fountain_profile.tres)) schedules health and mana fountains across the whole run. Each `FountainKind` draws two or three fountains from the run seed alone, so every floor agrees without shared state. Depths 2 to 24 split into equal bands, one fountain per band, and kinds avoid sharing a floor. A floor's fountain uses that kind's stream to pick one of its four designs and a free-standing room tile. Tiles walkable on every side are preferred, connector-adjacent tiles are skipped, and the placement keeps loot spacing and arrival clearance. Like chests, a fountain is a solid navmesh obstacle that must stand on reachable floor, keep door swings clear and leave no inaccessible pocket. A cramped floor that cannot fit a fountain logs it and the run has one fewer.

`Fountain` grants its `FountainBlessing` strategy (`RestoreHealthBlessing`, `RestoreManaBlessing`) once, through the ordinary timed interaction. A drinker whose pool is full sees a blocked prompt. Drinking hides the art's `Liquid` node, leaving the basin empty. The [pool effects](world-effects.md#fountain-effects) spray a burst over the change, and the drinker plays the healing or mana burst. The drink also plays the drinker's use-item gesture: an interaction offer can name an animation action, and `Player.CurrentActionId` falls back to it while the interaction runs. The fountain's `depth:N:fountain:<kind>` id becomes one of the run's cleared entities, so a revisited or loaded floor rebuilds it drained.

## Room geometry and stairs

Every room is a `Room` with floor, wall and decoration GridMaps built by its room type. GridMap cells are one world unit with centering disabled. Logical map tiles are **4×4 units centered on their anchor**, even when floor meshes are smaller or larger. The room owns its doorways completely: assembly hands the room its tile map as data (footprint tiles as floor, stairwell and pool pits as chasms, each doorway as a connector facing outward) and fills every doorway right away with its wall style's door, its opening frame or nothing (an open walkway); every other edge is walled. Nothing re-derives the map from geometry, and nothing adds, swaps or removes a doorway's door later: the room hands each door to the floor with the doorway it fills, and a doorway the corridors cannot reach fails generation. Before furnishing, assembly rejects a candidate when its inferred wall regions overlap. MeshLibrary item transforms are included; compound wall regions follow the standard junction footprints. The generator rotates complete rooms in quarter-turns: GridMaps, props and doors as geometry, the tile map as data. Room and floor wall generation resolve edge junctions with corners, T pieces and crossings, retaining compatible straight wall variants. If corridor walls cannot fit around a room-owned divider, generation retries the layout without committing overlapping pieces. Door frames and secret disguises reserve their 4×1 footprint; 1.5-unit wall pieces fit adjoining perpendicular walls against the frame face. Frames add no permanent collision, keeping the full doorway clear when the door is open. Assets must conform to their structural footprints and seam positions; see the [remaining wall-piece plan](specs/exact-wall-placement-plan.md).

The level's stairs are scenes ([up](../scenes/levels/stairs/stairs_up.tscn), [down](../scenes/levels/stairs/stairs_down.tscn)) placed by the entrance and exit room types' `StairsStep`: the staircase with its collision, the arrival `PlayerSpawnPoint` and the `LevelTransitionTrigger`. Up-stairs sit flush against the wall directly behind their top, including on wall runs with deeper pillars elsewhere. Down-stairs sink into a two-tile stairwell cut into open floor with floor on every side. Continuous stone walls line both sides one floor below, with their tops flush with the floor and the original fence above. The walk-on end and the exit at the bottom remain open. The room maps it as an enclosed chasm. Either way the landing at the walk-on end (the up-stairs' foot, the down-stairs' top, flush with the floor) is claimed for the rest of the assembly: no floor prop may cover it and every doorway must keep reaching it. Generated loot, keys and traps keep clear of every landing, and floor reachability floods from the arrival's `Landing`.

Corridor floor materials and wall styles validate before room generation. Missing assignments or invalid tiles report a generation failure and return to the menu, clearing the transition fade.

## Room assembly

Garrison rooms are assembled with their content by a `RoomType` ([room types](../scenes/levels/rooms/room_types/)): one strategy, or weighted palette of strategies, per axis. `RoomAssembler` runs them in a fixed order — shape → doorways → walls → circulation → columns → furnishing steps → floor → props — each axis on its own derived stream, so changing a floor style or toggling columns never moves furniture. Rooms are finished before floor packing and rotate as a whole; gameplay must connect every doorway, as before. A required step that cannot fit retries with a new shape (`Attempts`); a type that never fits throws instead of delivering a different room, naming the step and the last placement it refused. The [authoring guide](../scenes/levels/generators/assembly/README.md) covers adding props, groups, styles, steps and room types.

| Axis | Strategies |
| --- | --- |
| Shape and size | `RectangleShape` (optional odd width), `LShape`, `TShape`, `UShape`, `CrossShape`; width across, length along the room axis (+Z) |
| Doorways | `MinDoorways`/`MaxDoorways`, `DoorwayPlacement` (any, long-axis ends, long sides, a single end that leaves the far wall for a focal point), `SecretDoorChance` per doorway (a secret always creates a door), `SecretDetection`, `DoorChance` for the rest (1 always, 0 never), then `FrameChance` for the wall style's opening frame; the rest are open walkways |
| Circulation | Doorway approaches or a door-to-door spine kept free of furniture |
| Walls | `WallStyle`: weighted straight pieces and accents, plus doorway `DoorScene` and `Opening`; the boundary graph uses shared corner, T, crossing, half and short junction pieces and retains a chosen straight variant when its footprint fits. Wider accents and pillars are kept when their footprints do not intersect other pieces; otherwise they give way to plain walls |
| Floor | `UniformFloor`, `BorderedFloor`, `SplitFloor` over `FloorMaterial`s (weighted slabs, quarter pieces and wear accents); a split runs one straight or diagonal boundary across the room, never scattered patches; `FloorInlay`s set drains, gutters and water channels into the tiling; an optional `FloorRug` lays a runner down the middle or a bordered rug over the open floor, without collision, so spawns and loot still use its tiles |
| Columns | `AisleColumns`, `PerimeterColumns`, `CenterColumns`, `CornerColumns`, behind `ColumnChance`; symmetric groups place all-or-nothing |
| Furnishing | `AlongWallsStep`, `CornersStep`, `RowsStep`, `CenterStep`, `ScatterStep`, `WallDecorStep`, `CellsStep` (barred cells with gates), `FencedAreaStep` (bays, walkways, dividers), `PoolStep` (water sunk into the floor) and `ChoiceStep` (one weighted variant); a step with `MinimumCount` is required, `Chance` makes it occasional and `Uniform` keeps one content choice per room (one banner colour) |

Steps place `Placeable`s. A `Prop` is one object: mesh-library items (cosmetic variants) or scenes for props that need nodes, measured from the model. Its front faces local +Z; back-to-wall props stand with -Z on the wall face, wall props hang on the mounting surface at `MountHeight`. A `PropGroup` is an anchor plus optional members at offsets (a bed with nightstand and chest, a barrel with a side table); `SeatedTable` spaces one kind of seat along a measured table (chairs or benches, spaced by their width). Every piece is validated on its own, so a member that does not fit is dropped, and pieces may rest on others (plates and candles on a table). Weighted picks are balanced over the room: counts follow the weights, while equal options alternate instead of repeating; unused variants come first. Loose props carry a small seeded turn and shift (`TurnJitter`, `ShiftJitter`) so barrels, stools, vases and tableware sit naturally off the packing grid.

`RoomBlueprint` checks every piece for floor support, wall and prop overlap (with each prop's `Gap`) and circulation, and keeps all doorways mutually reachable on a 0.5-unit `WalkabilityGrid` sized for the largest ordinary walking body (`RoomAssembler.ActorRadius`, 0.6, the skeletons; the player's 0.5 capsule fits wherever they do). Inaccessible pockets behind furniture are allowed up to the type's `MaxPocketShare` of standable floor (default 10%). Interactive props reserve the space in front of them. Walkability tests nine sample points per cell (a quarter-cell lattice) against the full actor radius, so a gap is open exactly when the actor fits through it, wherever the grid falls: a 2.4-unit gate admits a 2-unit actor, a 1.7-unit one does not. Assembled props are plain nodes under the room's `Props` (mesh plus static collision on the prop layer, so they block actors and navigation but not exploration sight). Stretched structural pieces such as cell bars get box colliders sized to their stretch. Prison gates are ordinary doors with [interior identities](door-access.md#interior-cell-gates).

Fitted structure can mark room-local floor patches for the later cosmetic pass. The shared prison recipe uses `CellsStep.CellFloors` to choose brown or gray dirt for all its cell rows, independently of the stone or brick aisle. Patches align to the quarter-tile grid and use a separate cosmetic RNG stream. Patches do not change furniture placement or walkability. The [room generation harness](../scenes/debug/README.md#room-generation) previews the production prison, depot and treasury alongside the other realm rooms. The Garrison treasury has one entrance, secret 80% of the time, and a chest containing 240 gold.

Water is impassable to the player and walking monsters. `PoolStep` cuts whole tiles out of the floor and fills them with pool pieces: quay walls around the perimeter, open water between tiles. Scattered pools keep a dry border; cistern halls always have a centered pool with dry routes around it. Drain tunnels keep a dry central walkway and crosswalks at both ends, with water extending to the room walls on the left, right, or both sides. Every layout preserves doorway connectivity. Flying traversal is not implemented or verified.

The room maps water as chasm tiles, so it reveals with the room but cannot host walking actors, loot, or traps. Actor-only barriers on the `walking_terrain` physics layer block entry even without fencing; walking actor scenes include that layer in their masks, while projectile and sight queries ignore it. Monster navigation treats the pits as off the floor. Optional railings stand along dry banks and are omitted where they would obstruct circulation. Walkable floor inlays use grates rather than open-water visuals. Every water surface is drawn by the [water shader](../scenes/levels/dungeon/water/water.gdshader): translucent at the surface, murky within about a unit of depth, pale where it meets stone or floating things.

### Corridor styling and decoration

Each realm's `DungeonTileFactory` pairs a `FloorMaterial` and `WallStyle` with an ordered array of `CorridorStep` strategies (`CorridorPassagewayStep`, `CorridorThresholdStep`, `CorridorCornerStep`, `CorridorHallwayStep`). Compatible room and corridor wall variants survive the shared boundary pass when their occupied footprints fit; conflicting variants fall back to plain walls, and straight and half pieces face inward (`WallFootprints.FacingInto`). After boundary walls are placed, `CorridorBlueprint` measures decoratable straight wall runs (excluding accents, corners, doorways and concealed secret-door walls), doorway thresholds, L-corners, T-junctions and straight 1-tile-wide spans, then executes each `CorridorStep` on its own derived decoration RNG stream and emits the accepted props under `CorridorProps`.

### Light flicker

Rooms and corridor palettes each expose `FixtureLitChance` (default 85%): generation chooses each fixture's initial state on a separate cosmetic seed, so lighting does not change placement. Dark-floor visibility still follows the environmental-light rules.

Every flickering light shares one Inspector-tunable `FlickerProfile` resource that turns noise into a brightness multiplier. Presets live in `scenes/effects/flicker_profiles/`: `torch` for wall torches, candles and the player's light, `flame` for fire spells and `electric` for lightning strobes. Each light starts its flicker at its own random offset, so the torches spawned with a floor never flicker in step. Standalone lights are `FlickeringLight` nodes, which flicker around their `Energy` only while visible; lights whose energy a spell effect already drives apply the flicker through `EffectLight`.

## Doors and enemy navigation

Monsters navigate on the floor's `ClearanceGrid`, not a navigation mesh. Once every solid is placed, generation measures the distance to the nearest solid on the same quarter-cell lattice as walkability, from the same tiles and collision outlines (inside the actor height band), and keeps each 0.5-unit cell's roomiest sample. A cell is traversable for a body radius when that clearance covers the whole radius, so one grid serves the rat, the skeletons and the Warden: each goes only where its body capsule physically fits, and steers toward the roomiest point of each cell. Every door, interior cell gates included, is a gate over its shut leaf's footprint; its frame and its open leaf are ordinary solids, so an open door still blocks at its jambs and beside the opening. Closed doors block physics, exploration sight and navigation; barred interior cell gates block traversal while allowing exploration into the cell. Opening or closing a door changes routes on the next query.

The level's `FloorNavigation` computes shared Dijkstra maps on the grid. A chase field per target and body size leads every chaser of that size downhill toward its target. Steps through cells tighter than the body radius plus a `NavigationProfile.PreferredMargin` cost more, so routes keep to the middle of corridors and doorways and round corners instead of cutting them, while open rooms still get direct lines. A flee field derived from it leads away from a threat and out of dead ends. Roaming, searching and ring points use bounded A* routes with the same costs. `NavigationComponent` aims at the farthest cell down the field that it can walk to in a straight line, heads straight for a nearby target once the line is clear, steers apart from other monsters (they never collide physically), and side-steps toward open space before reporting a stall. A monster whose `EnemyBehaviorProfile.StayInHomeRoom` is set (the Warden) moves anywhere in its spawn room but holds at its edge. Fields, routes and reachability are plain C#, so they are tested headless. The debug menu's Navigation overlay draws the grid. [Monster authoring](monster-authoring-design.md#awareness-and-pursuit) owns detection and pursuit rules.

Every walkable floor tile collides as one flat box at a shared height (0.05); surface detail and the slightly higher dirt and rubble tiles are visual only, so bodies never snag on seams between tiles. The player's body is a 0.5-radius capsule matching its model, skeletons 0.6. On stairs, a body pushing into a step with open space one step up is lifted over it, so slim bodies climb the 0.5-unit steps.

Fields are shared: every chaser of a similar body size follows one field per target, computed only as far out as its farthest chaser needs and refreshed when it goes stale or a door changes, so a crowd costs little more than a single monster. A monster makes progress only while its remaining route shrinks, so pacing in place counts as a stall.

Known limitations:

- **One plane:** the grid is 2D, so stairs and pits are not monster terrain; flying or swimming monsters would need their own traversal rule.
- **Static apart from doors:** only doors change the grid at runtime. A destroyed nest keeps its cells blocked until the floor is rebuilt.
- **Low obstacles:** anything rising into the actor height band (above 0.15) blocks every body, so curbs and pool rims stop rats as well.
- **Room-bound Warden:** releasing the Warden is a data change, but its 1.0-radius body is wider than the 1.57 units an open door leaves clear, so it would still pass only doorless openings.

## Map occlusion and fog of war

Permanent black caps hide void under the rotating isometric camera. Gameplay exploration uses bounded omnidirectional line of sight from the player, independent of room-entry events, facing, lighting and camera zoom. Walls, unsanctioned room/corridor edges and closed door thresholds block sight; open doorways reveal only their visible wedge. Exact diagonal corner crossings require both routes to be open. Furnishings do not block discovery in this first implementation. Editor previews expose generated space.

Discovery records an eight-by-eight sample grid per tile. Newly seen samples stay explored permanently, including across travel and save/load; unseen parts of a partially explored tile stay covered. A depth-based screen shader maps rendered surfaces back to this world-aligned history, feathers the boundary inward and fades newly discovered space into view. Feathering does not change authoritative discovery. There is no dimmed remembered-space state or current-line-of-sight requirement for already discovered content.

Exploration belongs to the level's `FloorDiscovery` node ([floor_discovery.tscn](../scenes/levels/floor_discovery.tscn), wired to `Level.Discovery`). `SightRadius` controls reach in world units (default 24), `FadeSeconds` how long newly seen space fades in (default 0.35 seconds) and `FeatherWidth` how far the fog feathers inward (default 1 world unit). Each floor owns its exploration texture and publishes it with the map bounds and fade clock as `fog_*` global shader uniforms ([fog_of_war.gdshaderinc](../scenes/levels/fog_of_war.gdshaderinc)), so the fog and the silhouettes above it share one coverage test. Sight updates at most ten times per second while moving, and refreshes after door changes; a closed door seals its threshold because sight reads the door itself. The fade clock stops once a reveal has faded in. Exploration is independent of normal/dark-floor illumination.

Content gates retain tile-based discovery: discovering any sample on a tile enables its loot, effects and eligible x-ray silhouettes. The existing `ExplorationGateComponent` hides torch visuals, particles and lights until that tile is discovered. Previously discovered loot does not disappear when the player looks or moves away. The debug fog reveal fills the interior history, so gated loot, traps and effects appear with it; void remains capped.

Closed-door x-ray silhouettes appear only while the door is hidden (occluded, or in view on still-unexplored space; the fog's soft fringe over a door's explored face does not count), nearby and adjacent to revealed space. Unopened treasure chests use a golden occlusion silhouette on discovered tiles, controlled by the loot X-ray toggle; opening a chest or restoring it as opened removes the highlight. Both silhouettes draw above the fog, so walls still under fog do not hide them; their discovery gates keep them from revealing unexplored space. [Door access](door-access.md) adds concealed wall entrances, locks and colored key markers. Concealed doors expose no silhouette/prompt/marker; discovering a secret reveals a closed door, and opening it permits ordinary sight checks. Door access and explored samples persist independently; `Level.CaptureExploration` owns doors, traps and samples together. `SaveGame.CurrentVersion` rejects earlier development saves; no conversion is attempted.

Actors' visuals stop at walls. On the exploration sample grid (`MapData.WorldBounds`), `FloorDiscovery` also publishes the floor's `WallMask` as `floor_wall_mask`: samples inside solid wall pieces (corners as their two arms; visual-only doorway frames stay open) plus every closed door, concealed ones included, so clipping never singles out a secret wall. A `WallClipComponent` (instance [wall_clip_component.tscn](../scenes/components/wall_clip_component.tscn)) renders every mesh under the actor's visual root, gear attached later included, through a `WallClippedMaterial` that rebuilds the authored material on `wall_clipped.gdshader`, and gives each mesh the actor's center as its `wall_clip_anchor` instance parameter. A fragment whose top-down path from that anchor enters a wall sample is discarded ([wall_clip.gdshaderinc](../scenes/levels/wall_clip.gdshaderinc)): a leaning head, headdress or blade may sink into a wall but never shows beyond it, from any camera yaw. The anchor stays on the mesh, so skins, cloak, teleport, the hit flash and the death fade keep clipping; materials the shader cannot reproduce keep their authored material unclipped. The player and the Forsaken Warden carry the component; smaller monsters do not reach through walls and skip the per-mesh cost. Attack swooshes, weapon trails, item auras and monster x-ray silhouettes are separate meshes and are not clipped. The player's light stays on its side too: `PlayerLight` casts a ray from above the player's center to the light's spot ahead of the head each physics frame, and when a wall or door cuts it, moves the light to just in front of the hit, where the wall shadows the room beyond.

## Reachability

**Every active opening stays accessible, and nothing is placed where actors cannot reach.** After rooms, corridors and walls exist, the floor builds one `WalkabilityGrid` from the real collision (convex outlines, so corner walls keep corners walkable) and flood-fills it from the arrival with every door open. Enemy spawns, chests, traps, loose loot, keys, teleport targets and drop landings only use reachable cells; a chest is rejected if it would cut off any reachable space. Large actors additionally need reachable cells across their footprint. Inaccessible pockets may exist inside rooms within their type's budget; they simply never receive content. Actual movement follows the [navigation grid](#doors-and-enemy-navigation) built from the same collision.
