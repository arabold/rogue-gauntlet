# Stats and itemization

Implemented item ownership, inventory and stat rules. Godot Resources supply definitions and tuning; [item identification](item-identification-system.md) owns hidden type knowledge, the [gameplay catalog](item-catalog.md) owns content references, and [randomness and seeds](randomness-and-seeds.md) owns seeded reconstruction. Equipment, armor and curse numbers come from the [balance model](balance-model.md#gear) through `simulate.py derive`, which writes them into the `.tres` files; this document names the rules, not the values.

## Attributes and modifiers

Player stats are copied per actor. Strength, Dexterity, Vitality and Intelligence feed secondary stats through an authored `StatProfile`; class profiles choose their coefficients. Attributes scale shared curves by percent around a baseline of 10, so a class's strengths keep their proportion at every level: Vitality scales max health (base plus `HealthPerLevel` per level), Strength scales an attack's damage range, Intelligence takes Strength's place for a wand's or staff's missiles and spells, and Dexterity adds critical chance per point. The base damage on the stat sheet is the bare hands' hit; a weapon replaces it. Modifiers apply flat contributions before percentage contributions. Equipment registers contributions under the item instance and a buff under each activation, so removing one source reverses its effects without changing another's, even two overlapping drinks from one potion stack. Monsters use their local `ActorStatsComponent` for the same modifier ledger without acquiring player progression state.

A weapon's damage-range and critical-chance modifiers apply only to attacks made with that weapon: its own scaled damage and crit, rolled sharpness/ferocity/keenness affixes and a dread curse alike. `PlayerStats.ResolveAttack(weapon)` folds them into the same sums as the character's modifiers, while `MinDamage`/`MaxDamage`/`CritChance` give the unarmed values. Two worn weapons therefore never add their damage together. A weapon's other modifiers, such as attribute affixes, and damage modifiers on other gear or buffs apply to every attack. The run menu's Gear tab shows the main-hand weapon's attack.

Supported secondary stats include maximum health, maximum mana, speed, dodge distance, damage range, critical chance and armor. Their base values and the base attributes are authored on the player's stat sheet ([`player_stats.tres`](../scenes/player/resources/player_stats.tres)); a class then overwrites the four attributes. Dexterity does not grant passive accuracy or evasion. Resolved values drive health, movement, damage and the HUD; maximum-health derivation must not be fed back into base stats. See [contact resolution](combat-system.md#contact-resolution) for hit handling.

Timed stat buffs expire once; permanent buffs remain until explicitly removed. Periodic healing restores fixed points per tick (`HealingBuff.HealthPointsPerTick`), so a healing potion or food matters less as the hero's health grows and larger potions arrive deeper (see the [balance model](balance-model.md#the-hero)); poison takes whole points per tick past armor, from the same model.

## Experience and levels

Killing a monster pays XP from its threat level (see [the balance model](balance-model.md#cost-threat-level-and-xp)). XP climbs the shared ladder in [`leveling_profile.tres`](../scenes/player/resources/leveling_profile.tres): each level adds `HealthPerLevel` and the class's attribute growth (`StatProfile.*PerLevel`; fractions accumulate). A level-up raises current health by the same amount as max health, so it never heals damage already taken. `PlayerStats.LeveledUp` fires once per level; the player plays the level-up effect and `SignalBus.PlayerLeveledUp` reaches the HUD and log. Level and attributes are saved with the stats. Class starting profiles and gear are described in [character classes](character-classes.md).

## Body armor and helmets

Body armor (`Chest`) and helmets (`Head`) are `Armor` items in [scenes/items/armor/body/](../scenes/items/armor/body/) and [helmet/](../scenes/items/armor/helmet/), in light, medium and heavy `Weight`: body armor suits at tiers 1, 3 and 5 per weight, and the four classes' own hats at tier 1. Heavier pieces carry more armor. Because the game has no passive evasion, weight trades mobility instead: a heavy body piece slows its wearer and halves the dodge, a medium one shortens the dodge, and a light one quickens its wearer (`SpeedModifier`, `DodgeDistanceModifier`). Helmets keep full speed and the full dodge. Every class starts in T1 pieces of its weight (see [character classes](character-classes.md#the-shipped-roster)). Armor values are sized against the reference sword's hit at the depth each tier is on curve, so armor stays in the weapons' range; pieces of one slot, weight and tier are identical, so variety comes from quality and affixes. They roll the shield's table and drop from the armor loot pool in their tier's depth band. Worn, a suit goes onto the hero's skeleton in place of its arms and legs and a helmet mounts on the head bone (see [character classes](character-classes.md#the-model-swap)).

## Mana

Maximum mana is the stat sheet's `BaseMaxMana` plus `ManaPerIntelligence` per point of Intelligence plus `MaxMana` modifiers, so Intelligence gear deepens the pool. `ManaRegenRate` regenerates a fraction of the maximum per second, so a deeper pool also refills faster. The actor's `ManaComponent` holds current mana and regenerates it in gameplay time, processing only while below the maximum. The component owns current mana: `PlayerStatsController` pushes the maximum and regeneration into it, hands it the starting or saved value at spawn and after a load (once equipment and buffs have set the final maximum), and copies every change back into `PlayerStats.Mana`, which is saved beside health and shown by the HUD's mana bar. Lowering the maximum clamps current mana; raising it never refills. A new run starts full. A wand's or staff's spell spends it (see [weapon attacks](weapon-attack-system.md)); missiles are free.

Monster definitions provide their own fixed maximum and regeneration for innate spells. Each `MonsterSpellAction` chooses whether that activation pays its `SpellDefinition` cost; scroll and potion sources still pay through their own item-use rules, not the player's or monster's mana pool.

## Buffs and status conditions

A buff can declare the status it imposes in `Buff.Condition`, a `StatusCondition` Resource under `scenes/buffs/conditions/`. The condition file is the identity and is compared by reference, so always reference it as an external resource: `ItemFactory`'s deep duplicate copies embedded buffs but keeps external conditions shared. `BuffController.HasCondition` is true while any active buff carries the condition, so overlapping sources, such as poisoned food followed by a poison potion, read as one continuous condition. `BuffController` fires one `BuffsChanged` per application, removal, expiry sweep or `ClearBuffs`, and observers re-read what they need. Buffs target an actor-neutral `IBuffTarget`, implemented by players and monsters, so healing, timed poison and stat modifiers keep one runtime path; a stat modifier checks that the target supplies the required stat capability before applying.

Presentation, such as [screen effects](screen-effects.md) and [world effects](world-effects.md), and gameplay react to conditions for statuses without a magnitude, and to buff subclasses for effects with one:

- A plain `Buff` with only a `Condition` is a status with no behavior of its own, such as blindness (`blinded`) and confusion (`confused`). The blindness overlay and the player's `ConfusionComponent` react to those conditions.
- Actor status components turn conditions into behavior on players and monsters alike: `FrozenStatusComponent` (`frozen`: no actions or movement, animation held), `SlowStatusComponent` (`slowed_1`–`slowed_3`: slower movement), `BurningStatusComponent` (`burning`) and the hero's goo-bound hold. Each shows its look through the actor's world-effect host.
- `TimeScaleBuff` (Slowness) slows the whole game. The player's `TimeScaleComponent` applies the slowest active one to `Engine.TimeScale` and restores normal speed when the last one ends or the scene unloads. Durations count game time.
- `Buff.CuredConditions` ends conditions when any buff is applied; healing potions cure blindness and poison.

Attack payloads apply a timed buff saved as its own file, such as the Plague Rat's `rat_venom.tres`, only after a direct contact deals damage and the target survives (see [combat](combat-system.md)). With `Buff.Stacking = Refresh`, repeated applications of the same definition, from any source, keep one activation and its tick progress. Venom is the same periodic poison as other sources and imposes `poisoned`, so healing cures it too.

## Equipment quality, affixes and enchantment

Equipment has two independent properties:

1. **Base item.** The authored definition: kind, model and baseline values. Weapons, armor and jewelry with points carry a `Tier` (1–5) whose depth band fences where they drop; a higher tier gives more. Jewelry that changes speed or damage by a percentage is tier 0 and drops at every depth.
2. **Quality.** `ItemQuality` is rolled once at creation, stored on the instance and never secret, not even on unidentified jewelry. Poor and Superior scale base stats; Magic and Rare add affixes instead. One color per quality is shared by loot beams, slot tints, names and tooltips (`ItemQualityPalette`); orange is reserved for future Unique items.

| Quality | Color | Base stats | Affixes | Introduced at depth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Poor | grey | scaled down | 0 | 1 |
| Normal | white | as authored | 0 | 1 |
| Superior | white | scaled up | 0 | 1 |
| Magic | green | as authored | 1–2 | 2 |
| Rare | blue | as authored | 3–4 | 5 |

The odds, scale bands and affix counts live in [quality_ladder.tres](../scenes/items/quality/quality_ladder.tres). Each `QualityLevel` is introduced at `MinDepth` and its weight grows (or, for Poor, shrinks) linearly from there, never below zero. Later qualities simply outweigh earlier ones, so a Normal item can still drop deep. Depth mainly makes items better at what they are, through base tiers and affix tiers, rather than more often magical.

**Everything rolls at creation.** `LootRoller.ApplyRoll` draws from the source's loot RNG in a fixed order: quality, adjective, scale, affix count, affixes, curse, penalty, then the item's own `RollIntrinsic` draw. Only a wand or staff with a `SpellPool` uses that last draw, to pick its spell; other items draw nothing more. Nothing rolls later; copying, transferring, equipping or restoring an item never rerolls it. One scale applies to all of an item's intrinsic values, so its name and stats agree: weapon damage (whole points, the maximum never below the minimum), armor points, and every intrinsic jewelry modifier, harmful types included, so a superior Ring of Frailty is the worst one. Flat values keep a nonzero value at least 1 in magnitude; percentages and critical chance round to whole percents. Critical chance and armor speed penalties are not scaled. Fixed creation (starting gear, plain debug spawns) keeps Normal at a scale of exactly 1, so authored values show through unchanged.

**Roll tables.** Each definition references one `EquipmentRollTable` in [scenes/items/quality/tables/](../scenes/items/quality/tables/): ring, amulet, metal weapon, wooden weapon or shield (which body armor and helmets share). A table is plain authored arrays: `PoorAdjectives` and `SuperiorAdjectives`, one picked at random for those qualities, and `Prefixes` and `Suffixes` for Magic and Rare. The array an affix sits in decides whether it reads before or after the name. A new kind of equipment is a new table, not new code.

**Affixes.** Each `Affix` is one tier of a family, authored as its own file in [scenes/items/affixes/](../scenes/items/affixes/) so several tables can share it. Tiers open by depth (`MinDepth`), and every tier at or below the drop depth stays eligible and weighted, which keeps power creep slow. An item holds at most one affix per family (`Group`) and at most half its affix count, rounded up, of each kind: Magic gets one prefix, one suffix or both; Rare gets two of one kind and one or two of the other.

| Family | Kind | Adds | Rolled by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| sharpness ("Sharp") | prefix | flat damage | weapons |
| ferocity ("Savage") | prefix | damage % | weapons |
| keenness ("Keen") | prefix | critical chance | weapons, jewelry |
| plating ("Sturdy") | prefix | flat armor | shields, body armor, helmets, jewelry |
| warding ("Warded") | prefix | armor % | shields, body armor, helmets |
| strength, dexterity, vitality, intelligence ("of the Ox", "of the Fox", "of the Bear", "of the Owl") | suffix | attribute | every table |
| health ("of Vigor") | suffix | maximum health | every table |
| speed ("of Haste") | suffix | speed % | shields, body armor, helmets, jewelry |

**Names.** Poor and Superior lead with their adjective ("Cracked Broadsword", "Polished Broadsword"); Normal shows the base name alone. Magic and Rare wrap the base name in their first prefix and first suffix ("Vicious Broadsword of the Ox"); further affixes add stats but no name. A wand's or staff's spell is part of its base identity and takes the first suffix's place in every name, identified or not: "Wand of Fireball", "Vicious Ruby Scepter Wand of Meteors", "Magic Wand of Fire Nova"; its suffixes still add stats. Saves store the spell's definition path. Identified jewelry reads `{adjective or first prefix} {true name} {signed primary value}`, e.g. "Ring of Strength +3", "Shiny Ring of Strength +4" or "Keen Ring of Strength +3". Unidentified jewelry leads with its quality word: "Shiny golden ruby ring", "Magic golden ruby ring" (see [identification](item-identification-system.md#model-and-size-two-independent-channels)). Base names carry no condition words, since the quality names the condition.

An `ItemCreationPolicy` chooses fixed creation or randomized equipment/food initialization; rolling equipment requires its `QualityLadder` and a roll table on the definition. Starting supplies use fixed creation; dungeon sources use an explicit policy and RNG.

Enchantment adds one affix from the item's table, eligible at the current depth and never stacking a family it already holds. The item becomes Magic or Rare to match its affix count, dropping any Poor or Superior adjective but keeping its base scale; an item at Rare's maximum is not a valid target. Equipped targets are safely rebound so resolved stats reflect the changed item.

Magic and Rare items drop with hidden attributes. They read "Magic Broadsword" (green) or "Rare Broadsword" (blue) and show their quality but no stat lines. Wearing them, reading Identify on them or enchanting them reveals everything (see [per-item knowledge](item-identification-system.md#per-item-knowledge)).

**Curses.** Any equipment can carry a hidden curse, rolled at creation like everything else.
- **Chance:** the creation policy's `CurseChance` applies independently of quality, so trying unknown gear on is a real gamble. Harmful types (`AlwaysCursed`: Ring of Frailty, Ring of Lethargy, Amulet of Dread) are always cursed; fixed creation, gatekeeper drops and shop stock never are.
- **Penalty:** every cursed item, harmful types included, carries one hidden penalty, picked from the policy's `CursePenalties` and stored as the item's `CursePenalty` modifiers (an item is cursed exactly when it has them). These are unnamed affixes in [scenes/items/affixes/curses/](../scenes/items/affixes/curses/): −health, −Strength, −Dexterity, −armor, −damage or −speed %. Penalties come in tiers 1–5 that roll in the gear tier bands (an affix's `MinDepth` and `MaxDepth`), each worth up to twice a ring of its tier (dread, on damage, up to three times), so a curse can outweigh the item it sits on. Speed is a percentage everywhere on gear, so its penalty is untiered. The penalty applies while worn and shows in the HUD's stats, but the tooltip lists it only once the curse is revealed.
- **Sticky:** once worn, a cursed item cannot be unequipped, displaced by equipping something else (including the other hand of a two-handed combination), dropped or destroyed. `Inventory` returns `ItemFailure.Cursed` without changing anything, and names the blocking item as the result's subject.
- **Revealed:** equipping a cursed item reveals its curse at once, with a curse flash on screen and a log line; so does the player's blocked attempt to remove it, and the scroll of identify. A revealed curse leads the name ("Cursed Vicious Broadsword") and the tooltip shows "Cursed" with the penalty; a known clean item shows "Uncursed".
- **Lifted:** Enchantment lifts the curse from its target. So does the Scroll of Remove Curse, which targets one item, worn or not. Any item not known to be uncursed is a valid target, and both leave the item known as uncursed, so neither reveals whether a curse was there. Both change the item in place through `Inventory.ChangeItem`; a worn item stays worn and only its stats are re-registered.

## Loot eligibility

Source tables own category budgets; shared loot pools in [scenes/items/loot/pools/](../scenes/items/loot/pools/) own each category's depth bounds and quantities. Items do not infer drop eligibility from their model name. Grouped loot selects an eligible category before a variety inside it, so adding weapon variants cannot displace the authored recovery-supply budget. Simple tables may use flat entries; do not author flat and grouped forms together.

A source's `LootDropProfile` ([scenes/items/loot/drops/](../scenes/items/loot/drops/)) decides its table, drop chance, roll count and creation policy. A monster drops only what it could carry: beasts such as rats, bats and snakes use the [beast profile](../scenes/items/loot/drops/beast.tres), an occasional piece of raw meat that is often tainted; skeletons, orcs and other humanoids roll the [common enemy table](../scenes/items/loot/loot_enemy_common.tres) of gold, potions, food, scrolls and gear, sometimes, while skeleton warriors and orc captains always drop. Floors carry most loot instead (see [level design](level-design.md#loot)): ordinary chests and loose finds roll the [common chest table](../scenes/items/loot/loot_chest_common.tres) once, locked rooms' gold chests the [uncommon table](../scenes/items/loot/loot_chest_uncommon.tres) and secret rooms' chests the [rare table](../scenes/items/loot/loot_chest_rare.tres) two or three times, and gold piles lie on the floor. Gold piles grow with depth and vary in size (`LootTableItem.QuantityPerDepth`, `QuantitySpread`).

Gatekeepers always drop a weapon of their realm's tier from their own table, as a Diablo II boss drops items of its own level, only of better quality: the Forsaken Warden ([table](../scenes/enemies/forsaken_warden/forsaken_warden_loot.tres)), the Prison's Minotaur ([table](../scenes/enemies/minotaur/minotaur_loot.tres)) and the Sewer's Gelatinous Cube ([table](../scenes/enemies/gelatinous_cube/gelatinous_cube_loot.tres)). They roll under the [gatekeeper policy](../scenes/items/gatekeeper_item_policy.tres), whose ladder never rolls below Magic and never curses, and introduces Rare at the same depth as ordinary drops, so the first gatekeeper's drop is usually Magic and only sometimes Rare.

Weapon, armor and jewelry base tiers are fenced by depth through [`tier_depth_bands.tres`](../scenes/items/quality/tier_depth_bands.tres), which `simulate.py derive` writes from the balance model's `tier_intro` and `tier_band`. Each tier enters with its realm (tier 1 at depth 1, tier 2 with the Prison at depth 6, and so on every five depths) and keeps dropping for eight depths, into the next realm's first floors, so neighbors overlap and smooth the transition; tier 5 never stops. The weapon, armor and jewelry pools reference the bands (`LootPool.TierBands`), so each equipment entry drops in its item tier's band and stores no window of its own; tier 0 drops at every depth, and other entries keep their own window. Every weapon, shield and armor piece's numbers come from its kind, slot, weight and tier, so items of one kind and tier differ only in look, quality and affixes. Monster threat and item tier are selection and tuning concepts, not automatic depth-based stat scaling. See the [catalog](item-catalog.md#source-budgets-and-eligibility) and the source `.tres` files for actual eligibility and weights.

## Portable-light equipment

Six `PortableLightItem` definitions provide held illumination in the off hand. Each assigns a `HeldScene` and a `ShieldHandGrip` adapter; the ordinary `Scene` is the unlit pickup and inventory model. Equipping a light replaces a shield, or a two-handed weapon, which needs the off hand; equipping a shield or a two-handed weapon puts the light away. Existing curse checks keep these transfers atomic, and displaced gear stays in the backpack. Saves keep the item and its hand; restoring equipment recreates the held light. A held light also gives a makeshift guard (see [off-hand defenses](weapon-attack-system.md#off-hand-defenses)).

Holding a light attaches the lit model and enables the forward light on `PlayerLight`, on every floor. On a dark floor without one, the hero sees only a small circle around them (see [screen effects](screen-effects.md) and [lighting and atmosphere](level-design.md#lighting-and-atmosphere)). Lights burn without fuel or manual ignition. They are spawned from the debug menu (**Items → Lamps and torches**) and kept out of normal loot and quality rolls until supply and fuel are designed.

## Item ownership, food, and persistence

Authored definitions remain shared and immutable. Every runtime individual or homogeneous stack owns its Resource, definition provenance and instance identity. Equipment references inventory entries rather than a second copy. Whole-item transfers preserve identity; splitting creates another record, and merging retires the source record.

The backpack holds 20 entries, including equipped gear; larger backpacks are not implemented. Currency remains in player stats. Potions, scrolls, keys and lockpicks stack to 20 of the exact same definition; equipment and food remain individual. Capacity is saved. Compatible merges can succeed in a full backpack without exceeding a stack cap.

Pickup, use, drop, purchase and equipment operations go through `PlayerInventoryController`, which validates ownership, quantity, targets and space before committing. Failed pickup preserves the world source; a successful use applies one effect and consumes one unit. UI and quick slots use the same ownership rules. Items leave the pack only through the same controller: a sale to a [shop](shops.md), or theft. A thief's first damaging hit takes one random stack the hero is not wearing; killing the thief before it escapes drops the stack at its feet, saved with the floor like any dropped item. Identification knowledge does not determine stack equality.

Food recovery and contamination are per-piece state. Dungeon creation may roll contamination once; fixed starting supplies and shop stock do not. Copying or loading preserves it. Food has no type-wide disguise, stacking cannot disclose contamination, and there is no hunger system.

Inventory restoration validates the complete state before replacement and preserves quantities, quality, adjective, value scale, affixes, curse and penalty and what is known about them, food state and equipment references. Active player buffs are saved with their remaining time and tick progress; holds that last only while the hero stands in something (a slime pool, an area spell) are left out. Floors regenerate their untouched baseline and restore saved differences; living enemies reset on revisit. Current-version compatibility is defined by `SaveGame.CurrentVersion`; older development saves require a new run, without conversion or silent overwrite.

## Authoring guide

- Add buffs through `StatModifierBuff` and its modifier list; set duration to distinguish timed and permanent effects. Reference conditions as external resources.
- Tune quality odds, introduction depths, base-stat bands and affix counts in `quality_ladder.tres`. Add affix tiers as files and list them in the roll tables that may roll them, rather than in hardcoded gameplay paths.
- Assign an `EquipmentRollTable` on every equipment definition and keep condition words out of base names.
- Create item definitions with explicit stack limits, effects, slots, tier and visual wrapper. Assign creation policy and loot eligibility at the source. Regenerate equipment numbers with `simulate.py derive` rather than editing them by hand.
- Verify handedness and held scale on the actual player rig. See [equipped orientation](item-catalog.md#equipped-orientation); floating previews are insufficient.
- Use the Remote Inspector to inspect runtime copies. Use supported equipment/enchantment commands instead of editing live modifier collections without rebinding.

Exercise creation, equipment, food, stacks and capacity in the game or the [combat harness](combat-harness.md) through the debug menu: its **Items** submenu spawns any authored item, dropped, packed or equipped as **Delivery** says, and **Fill backpack** packs food until the backpack is full. Its **Quality** picker rolls spawned equipment like a drop, at a random or chosen quality, and its **Cursed** toggle forces a curse. Each wand or staff opens a list of the spells its pool can roll, so it spawns carrying the picked one. The **Spells** submenu casts any spell at a chosen level without a wand, free of mana and wind-up.
