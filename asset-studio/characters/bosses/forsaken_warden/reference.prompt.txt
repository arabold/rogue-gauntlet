Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: concept reference for a cohesive low-poly fantasy game asset library.

Style/medium: KayKit-inspired friendly, chunky low-poly 3D game art, matching the supplied project character references. Every asset should belong beside those characters in the same game. Treat references as style guides only; do not include their characters, equipment, or layouts unless the subject requests them.

Shape language: bold readable silhouette, exaggerated proportions, substantial thickness, broad planar facets, softly chamfered edges and intentionally low polygon curves. Rounded forms still have visible large facets. Use a strong primary form plus a few distinctive secondary forms. Include recognizable functional details at a scale visible from an elevated gameplay camera: collars, rolled rims, sockets, handles, chain links, cut faces or holes when appropriate. Simplify detail into purposeful modeled shapes; do not reduce the design to a stack of generic primitives.

Character proportions and fidelity: when the subject is a character, match the supplied hero reference closely: an oversized head or helmet about one third of total height, a compact torso, very short thick limbs, simple mitten-like hands and chunky feet. Large creatures and armored bosses gain size through world scale and breadth while retaining this stylized vocabulary. Avoid realistic adult anatomy, long legs, small heads and detailed collectible-figurine armor. Resolve armor into a few substantial plates and a handful of readable fittings, with deliberately simple bevels and broad facets.

Color and shading: restrained harmonious palette, clean solid color regions and subtle broad light-to-dark ramps like the project's character palette shading. Matte surfaces by default; stylized metal, glass or gems only where requested by the subject. Soft neutral illumination with gentle form shading and restrained contact shading. Material identity comes from color, shape and broad highlights. Keep surface colors clean and readable.

Construction: depict a plausible model with real volume in all three dimensions. Parts meet at convincing attachment points; chains connect through loops, handles attach to their supports, and objects rest naturally. No unexplained floating details or disconnected decoration. Views of the same asset must agree geometrically.

Presentation: plain white background, full object visible with comfortable margins. No scenery, dramatic lighting, depth of field, text, labels, logos, borders or watermark. The task-specific layout below controls views and pose.

Avoid: photorealism, food photography, realistic surface scans, tiny pores, speckles, crumbs, scratches, photographic wood grain, gritty wear, painterly brushwork, outlines, glossy product photography, cinematic reflections, dense sculpted detail and smooth high-poly realism. Preserve useful medium-scale detail and character rather than adding surface noise.

Task-specific instructions:
Image 1 shows two renders of an original KayKit large brute character, one posed with weapons and one in a T-pose. It is the target for body proportions, build, limb lengths, facet size and surface finish only; do not copy its orc, skulls, bones, necklace, war paint, hair, axe, knife or colors. Image 2 is our earlier Forsaken Warden concept: keep its identity, armor motifs, palette and equipment, but discard its proportions. Image 3 is our project's approved Orc Warrior concept and confirms the rendering and finish of characters in this game.

Proportions override the hero rule above. This is a boss brute, not a hero: rebuild the Warden on the build of image 1. The helmet is about one quarter of the total standing height and sits low and slightly forward between two huge shoulder masses, with no visible neck; the tops of the pauldrons rise to the level of the visor slit. The shoulder span across both pauldrons is close to the full standing height. A massive barrel chest and heavy belly take about two fifths of the height. Very long, very thick arms with forearms and fists larger than the upper arms; with the arms hanging, the fists reach the upper thighs. Short thick legs set wide apart, about a quarter of the height, ending in broad heavy sabatons. Simple oversized mitten fists. The overall silhouette is a wide, top-heavy trapezoid.

Subject: the Forsaken Warden, a huge revenant jailer knight guarding the Sunken Garrison. Muted charcoal blue-gray steel plate with sparse dull brass trim. A fully enclosed blocky greathelm with one simple horizontal visor slit, two small glowing amber eye lights, a few vertical breathing slots, and a low brass crown-shaped rim around its flat top; no face, beard, plume or horns. A thick steel gorget collar joins the helmet to the chest. A keeled breastplate with one simple brass sunburst with a dark-red center, and two broad belly bands below it. Two large single-plate rounded pauldrons with brass edging and one lower lame each, each held to the breastplate by a visible leather strap and buckle. Simple thick steel vambraces over the forearms and armored mitten gauntlets. A wide brown leather belt with one chunky square brass buckle at the front. A dark oxblood cloth panel hangs from the belt at front and back between two curved steel hip plates. Blocky knee guards with a brass rim, thick plated shins and broad sabatons. A short curved ivory war horn with brass bands hangs from a leather strap looped through the belt on his left hip. Two or three large modeled nicks in the plates suggest old service. Every plate overlaps or is strapped to the plate or body beneath it; nothing floats.

Equipment, shown only on the left figure: a very broad thick steel executioner sword with a squared blunt tip, a short simple brass crossguard and a long dark wrapped handle with a brass pommel, held in his right fist; a thick heater shield with a muted red face, a steel rim and one chunky brass sun emblem, strapped to his left forearm.

Layout: wide clean landscape character concept sheet with three figures of the exact same character at the same scale, standing on the same floor line. Left: a relaxed three-quarter combat stance holding the sword and shield. Middle: a direct front view in a modeling A-pose with empty closed fists, arms angled about 25 degrees from vertical so the gap between arms and torso is visible, legs apart, shoulders level. Right: a direct side profile of the same A-pose, facing the left edge of the picture, empty fists. Orthographic camera at body mid-height, soft neutral light, white background, comfortable margins, no text. The result should look like screenshots of one finished, flat-colored, low-polygon game mesh.
