# Characters, helmets, and body armor

Use this workflow for new enemies, helmets, body armor, and future heroes. The [Orc Warrior](characters/orc/orc_warrior/README.md) is the first approved production example. Food, potions, weapons, shields, and props continue through the [item workflow](README.md#standalone-item-workflow).

## Approve the design and turnaround

1. Gather actual KayKit game assets of similar proportions and complexity, the project's art direction, and the subject brief. Use their silhouette, facet size, construction and palette as references for an original design. Approve the concept before reconstructing it.
2. Create aligned orthographic front, left, back, and right views of the same design. Match pose, scale, framing, and asymmetric details across views. Use empty hands for a character whose weapon comes from the game. Inspect the four views together, then split them into clean individual inputs in the order the reconstruction job expects.
3. Save the selected concept, turnaround and exact prompts with the source. Use the [shared style prompt](prompts/stylized-fantasy.txt) as input and keep subject-specific decisions beside the asset.

## Reconstruct the shape and retopologize in Blender

- Start with untextured multi-view image-to-3D and request separate mesh parts. The Orc used a direct Tripo H3.1 job. Use the provider's tool or API for this step. Record the inputs, provider, model version, view order and settings in the source's `provenance.json`, as the [Orc's](characters/orc/orc_warrior/provenance.json) does; export refuses a Blender source without one. Keep the dense result in temporary storage as a shape reference. Register it to the correct scale, ground plane, front direction, and rig before fitting clean geometry.
- Keep the editable mesh organized by anatomical region or physical part. Separate rigid and reusable pieces such as the helmet, shoulder plates, belt hardware, wraps, and boots. Build continuous skin and edge loops through the chest, shoulders, and elbows where a generated split would open or pinch in motion. Inspect how separate parts seat against their actual supports.
- Allocate triangles by surface and motion. Refit organic parts with edge flow around joints and retain the forms that define the silhouette. Rebuild hard pieces with clean planes, deliberate bevels, and simple fastening geometry. Center rivets on their straps and seat hardware flush. Compare front, back, side and three-quarter silhouettes to the turnaround and the reconstruction.
- Use a comparable KayKit asset as the triangle baseline. Measure the triangulated export. Spend additional triangles where silhouettes, visible bevels, or deformation need them. The Orc's first target was about 4–5k triangles. Its final body uses 6,002 and its axe adds 274.

## Scripted reconstruction

`studio.py reconstruct` scripts the steps above for a humanoid on the KayKit skeleton, a creature on one of the creature rigs, or a rig authored in the config; the [Nymph](characters/nymph/nymph/README.md), the [acid blob](characters/slime/acid_blob/README.md), the [giant bat](characters/bat/giant_bat/README.md), the [chest mimic](characters/mimic/chest_mimic/README.md), the [snake](characters/snake/snake/README.md), the [Forsaken Warden](characters/bosses/forsaken_warden/README.md) and the [minotaur](characters/minotaur/minotaur/README.md) were built this way. Each command works on one character folder that already holds the approved `reference.png`, a `turnaround.prompt.txt`, a `provenance.json` with its `reconstruction` settings and a `blender_source` recipe.

```bash
./.venv/bin/python asset-studio/studio.py reconstruct turnaround nymph/nymph --count 2   # paid image drafts
./.venv/bin/python asset-studio/studio.py reconstruct views nymph/nymph --image <selected draft>
./.venv/bin/python asset-studio/studio.py reconstruct tripo nymph/nymph                  # spends Tripo credits
./.venv/bin/python asset-studio/studio.py reconstruct parts nymph/nymph
./.venv/bin/python asset-studio/studio.py reconstruct build nymph/nymph
./.venv/bin/python asset-studio/studio.py reconstruct palette nymph/nymph                # recolor the saved source only
```

`views` splits the selected sheet into four views at one figure height, since image models rarely keep the figures at one scale. A view the image model drew from the wrong camera can be left out of the Tripo job with the provenance's `reconstruction.omit_views` (any view but the front), as the [snake's](characters/snake/snake/README.md) raised rear view was. `tripo` reads `TRIPO_API_KEY` from the repo `.env` and keeps the result in temporary storage. `parts` renders each reconstructed piece highlighted, so the character's [`reconstruction.json`](characters/nymph/nymph/reconstruction.json) can name them. `build` runs one fresh Blender and writes the recipe's source:

- `parts` joins pieces into named parts; `height`, `center_on` and `offset` register them in the rig frame.
- `drop_islands` (`{part: {"min": [x, y, z], "max": [x, y, z]}}`, in the rig frame's metres) leaves out a part's loose shells whose center lies inside the box, as the chain links hanging beside the minotaur's shackles.
- `budgets` sets each part's triangle count, decimated symmetrically for the parts in `symmetric` and island by island for the parts in `by_island` (overlapping lobes that shatter when decimated together).
- `joints`, `chains` and `rings` rebuild arms and legs as ring loops along their joint chains, so elbows and knees bend on clean loops.
- `smooth_angle` sets the hard-edge angle: 35° keeps a character's facets, 60° suits a soft slime.
- `palette`, `slots` and `regions` color each face from the turnaround view that sees it best, snapped to its part's slots. Face details such as eyes, brows and mouths only take their slot inside their region on the front of the face; a region's `half_width` may be `[inner, outer]`, a band either side of the middle for paired details such as tusks.
  A palette entry is an RGB triple shaded by the standard ramp, or `{"light", "base", "shadow"}` for a ramp that shifts hue. An entry's `match` is the tone the sheet draws when the game should show another: the [orc shaman's](characters/orc/orc_shaman/README.md) skull sockets match the sheet's mid-grey and render dark. Gate pale slots (tusks, bone, wraps, paint) with `regions` on a character whose skin the sheet draws pale and grey, since their shaded tones would match it.
  `undersides` (`{part: {"slot", "facing"}}`) gives faces turned down past `facing` a slot outright, since no turnaround view shows a belly from below.
  Palette entries are albedo, the colors the game lights. The turnaround draws them already shaded, so sampling its pixels gives swatches too dark for the game; brighten them to KayKit's levels (its steel sits near `[120, 130, 135]`) and let `shades` (value multipliers, such as `[0.5, 0.62, 0.75, 0.88, 1.0]`) match the sheet against each slot's lit and shadowed tones. `min_island` absorbs same-slot islands of fewer faces, the speckle shading and registration leave on broad plates.
- `rig` with `kind` "humanoid" moves the KayKit skeleton's joints to the landmarks while keeping its bone directions and rolls, so the template clips play unchanged. A recipe `rig` of `large` fits KayKit's large rig instead, the brute skeleton with a long torso, long arms and short legs, as the [Forsaken Warden](characters/bosses/forsaken_warden/README.md) does. The A-posed mesh is weighted on the posed skeleton, using the rules described in [`rig.weights`](blender/reconstruction/rig.py), and unposed into the rest pose. `kind` "creature" uses a rig from [`rigs/creatures/`](rigs/creatures/README.md) with its bone directions kept: the reconstruction is registered at the rig's size, and the recipe's `scale` sizes it for the game. The [acid blob](characters/slime/acid_blob/README.md) uses the blob rig unchanged. When the mesh differs from the rig's rest pose, `rig.fit` moves joints to new head positions (a bone left out moves with its fitted ancestor), and each `rig.chains` entry (`bones` and one `points` entry per bone plus the tip) keeps the chain's rest directions at the mesh's lengths, then bends the chain onto the mesh to weight it and unposes it. The [giant bat](characters/bat/giant_bat/README.md) fits its spread wings this way onto a rig that rests with them raised.
- `props` mounts procedural equipment (`prefab`, optional `args`) on the large rig's verified hand-slot `frame` from [`rigs/large/attachment_frames.json`](rigs/large/attachment_frames.json), skinned to that slot alone so the template clips swing it like KayKit's equipped weapons; `slots` maps each prefab material to a palette slot. Keep the turnaround empty-handed: a held weapon fuses with the fist and loses the slot's axes. The recipe lists each prop under `blender.attachments` to export it as its own mesh.
- `rig` with `kind` "authored" builds a rig no template provides from `rig.bones` (head, tail and parent per bone, in the source's metres), keys every clip from `rig.actions`, and its recipe sets `"rig": "authored"` with the bones as `required_bones`. The [chest mimic](characters/mimic/chest_mimic/README.md) is rigged this way. A `{"split": [lower, upper], "z": height}` weight rule puts a piece fused across a hinge on one bone below the height and the other above it.
- `rig.actions` keys clips the rig lacks, as poses per frame at 24 fps (bone-local `location`, `scale` and `rotation` in degrees; anything left out is at rest). They are baked to one linear key per frame, since the Studio exports linear samplers only. The acid blob's glide, melt and its held `Melted` pose are authored this way. `waves` adds sine rotations travelling down a bone chain on top of the keys (`bones`, `rotation`, `cycles`, `lag` in degrees per bone, optional `taper` and `envelope`); whole cycles loop seamlessly. The snake's slither, idle sway and death throes are waves on its spine. An action with `base` builds on one of the rig's clips instead of the rest pose: the clip is copied (`repeat` times end to end, for a longer cycle) and its keys become offsets, `rotation` turning each bone after the clip's own rotation and `location` adding to it, so the minotaur's head-down charge is the template run with the spine, chest and head pitched forward. List the base clip in `rig.clips`; the recipe's `clips` decide whether it ships too. A death clip that ends in a pose needs a one-key hold clip like `Melted` for the monster's dead state, or the dead state replays the death; `rig.holds` (`{"Dead": "Death"}`) keys one from a clip's last frame.

`palette` recolors a saved source from the current `palette` without the reconstruction, which `build` needs and temporary storage loses: the atlas layout depends only on the slots, so faces, UVs, weights and clips stay as built. It refuses a palette whose slots changed. The [giant bat](characters/bat/giant_bat/README.md) was brightened this way.

Review the build with `lineup`, then adjust the landmarks, budgets or palette and build again. Hand edits to the saved source are lost on the next build, so keep changes in `reconstruction.json` while the scripted build still serves.

## Shade with the project's palette approach

Use palette UVs to map faces and corners into a small shared gradient atlas. The Studio's [atlas builder](core/mesh.py) can produce the palette ramps. Choose swatches for the design and vary their shade across surfaces to show volume, cavities, and edges. Keep a distinguishable value range on steel, including the helmet, pauldrons, and fasteners. Keep metallic at zero like the KayKit comparators. Export makes non-metal surfaces matte (roughness 0.85) the way the game imports KayKit, so authored gloss between 0.25 and 0.85 does not survive; metal and deliberate gloss below 0.25 do. The baked axe carries its existing palette. Keep painted color variation in the atlas and use the stock game lighting for the final judgment.

## Rig and fit for the asset's role

- **Enemies.** Use the existing technical rig for the character type. For a humanoid, preserve the compatible bone names and hierarchy, grip sockets, and required clips. Fit the bind pose and weights to the model. Weight skin and soft garments through the joints. Bind hard armor to rigid bones or pivots, then bake hinge corrections into the clips. Bake the enemy's fixed armor and weapon into the model and verify the actual held asset on its socket.
- **Helmets.** Fit the sculpt and clean shell on the game head hosts in [`rigs/hosts/`](rigs/hosts/). Check the rim, ears, face, neck, and hair clearance on each supported head. Export in the existing head attachment frame and verify any hair or other covered parts on the equipped character.
- **Body armor.** Author the full worn body as a replacement mesh with its own arms and legs. Use the same hero rig and compatible bind pose for skinned armor. Start with the shared hero proportions and only make a second masculine or feminine body fit where it is needed. Keep both within the same stylized proportions. Export a separate static torso-only model for inventory and floor display. In Godot, verify equip and unequip, and check that the replacement hides precisely the base body parts it covers while retaining the head. Existing costume recipes use a different torso fit. The first new replacement suit needs its own in-game coverage check.
- **Future heroes.** Use the same modeling, palette and retopology process. Keep the base body, head and equipment coverage regions compatible with armor swaps. Preserve the shared hero skeleton and slots. The game equips hero helmets, armor, and weapons at runtime.

For an existing model replacement, preserve the required clip names, loop modes, attachment and node contracts, dimensions, GLB path and Godot UID. A stable bone name alone does not preserve the motion on a changed bind pose. Retarget and check the resulting motion. Keep only the clips the game plays: the monster scene's states and attacks plus its defense profile's clips. Baked clips are most of a character's file size, so bake another template clip onto the rig when a behavior needs it.

## Export and verify in Godot

1. Keep one current editable Blender source with named meshes, packed palette images, and any needed actions. Put raw reconstructions, interim meshes, draft exports, and review captures under the OS temporary work root. Export a staged self-contained GLB with the correct scale, axis conversion, skins, materials, and clips. Check that weights, normals, UVs and the intended texture survive import.
2. Install the reviewed GLB at the intended game art path. Preserve import settings and the UID when replacing existing art. Compose or update the actor or equipment scene separately. The [monster authoring guide](../docs/monster-authoring-design.md) covers behavior, collisions and other gameplay data.
3. Spawn the enemy in the [regular combat harness](../docs/combat-harness.md) at the default distance. Judge it at the actual game-camera angle, with the default lighting and the equipped weapon. Use close views to inspect fit. Review idle, walk, each gameplay attack and the transitions, and check shoulder plates, elbows, straps, head clearance and weapon grip. Sample intermediate frames around tight clearance. For wearable art, use the actual equipped hero and validate the separate display model in inventory or item preview. Confirm the game rig, render layers and materials before publishing a final catalog image.

Register a rigged humanoid with a `blender_source` recipe, following the [Orc recipe](characters/orc/orc_warrior/character.json). The Studio opens the saved Blender file in a separate process, builds a skinned GLB, and verifies the actual Godot import on export. Re-exporting an unchanged source writes byte-identical files. The recipe records the scene objects to export, the `clips` to ship (export fails when the source lacks one and drops any other action), animation roles and loop modes, and its `scale` (a number or `[width, height, depth]`) sizes the model against the heroes at the rig root without touching clips or weights. The Blender source owns the mesh, weights, palette images and actions. See [maintenance](maintenance/character-and-wearable-recipes.md) for earlier recipe builders.
