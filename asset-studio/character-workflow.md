# Characters, helmets, and body armor

Use this workflow for new enemies, helmets, body armor, and future heroes. The [Orc Warrior](characters/orc/orc_warrior/README.md) is the first approved production example. Food, potions, weapons, shields, and props continue through the [item workflow](README.md#standalone-item-workflow).

## Approve the design and turnaround

1. Gather actual KayKit game assets of similar proportions and complexity, the project's art direction, and the subject brief. Use their silhouette, facet size, construction and palette as references for an original design. Approve the concept before reconstructing it.
2. Create aligned orthographic front, left, back, and right views of the same design. Match pose, scale, framing, and asymmetric details across views. Use empty hands for a character whose weapon comes from the game. Inspect the four views together, then split them into clean individual inputs in the order the reconstruction job expects.
3. Save the selected concept, turnaround and exact prompts with the source. Use the [shared style prompt](prompts/stylized-fantasy.txt) as input and keep subject-specific decisions beside the asset.

## Reconstruct the shape and retopologize in Blender

- Start with untextured multi-view image-to-3D and request separate mesh parts. The Orc used a direct Tripo H3.1 job. Use the provider's tool or API for this step. Record the inputs, provider, model version, view order and settings in the source's `provenance.json`, as the [Orc's](characters/orc/orc_warrior/provenance.json) does; export refuses a Blender source without one. Keep the dense result in temporary storage as a shape reference. Register it to the correct scale, ground plane, front direction, and rig before fitting clean geometry.
- Keep the editable mesh organized by anatomical region or physical part. Separate rigid and reusable pieces such as the helmet, shoulder plates, belt hardware, wraps, and boots. Build continuous skin and edge loops through the chest, shoulders, and elbows where a generated split would open or pinch in motion. Inspect how separate parts seat against their actual supports.
- Allocate triangles by surface and motion. Refit organic parts with edge flow around joints and retain the forms that define the silhouette. Rebuild hard pieces with clean planes, deliberate bevels, and simple fastening geometry. Center rivets on their straps and seat hardware flush. Compare front, back, side and three-quarter silhouettes to the turnaround and the reconstruction.
- Use a comparable KayKit asset as the triangle baseline. Measure the triangulated export. Spend additional triangles where silhouettes, visible bevels, or deformation need them. The Orc's first target was about 4–5k triangles. Its final body uses 6,002 and its axe adds 274.

## Shade with the project's palette approach

Use palette UVs to map faces and corners into a small shared gradient atlas. The Studio's [atlas builder](core/mesh.py) can produce the palette ramps. Choose swatches for the design and vary their shade across surfaces to show volume, cavities, and edges. Keep a distinguishable value range on steel, including the helmet, pauldrons, and fasteners. Keep metallic at zero like the KayKit comparators. Export makes non-metal surfaces matte (roughness 0.85) the way the game imports KayKit, so authored gloss between 0.25 and 0.85 does not survive; metal and deliberate gloss below 0.25 do. The baked axe carries its existing palette. Keep painted color variation in the atlas and use the stock game lighting for the final judgment.

## Rig and fit for the asset's role

- **Enemies.** Use the existing technical rig for the character type. For a humanoid, preserve the compatible bone names and hierarchy, grip sockets, and required clips. Fit the bind pose and weights to the model. Weight skin and soft garments through the joints. Bind hard armor to rigid bones or pivots, then bake hinge corrections into the clips. Bake the enemy's fixed armor and weapon into the model and verify the actual held asset on its socket.
- **Helmets.** Fit the sculpt and clean shell on the game head hosts in [`rigs/hosts/`](rigs/hosts/). Check the rim, ears, face, neck, and hair clearance on each supported head. Export in the existing head attachment frame and verify any hair or other covered parts on the equipped character.
- **Body armor.** Author the full worn body as a replacement mesh with its own arms and legs. Use the same hero rig and compatible bind pose for skinned armor. Start with the shared hero proportions and only make a second masculine or feminine body fit where it is needed. Keep both within the same stylized proportions. Export a separate static torso-only model for inventory and floor display. In Godot, verify equip and unequip, and check that the replacement hides precisely the base body parts it covers while retaining the head. Existing costume recipes use a different torso fit. The first new replacement suit needs its own in-game coverage check.
- **Future heroes.** Use the same modeling, palette and retopology process. Keep the base body, head and equipment coverage regions compatible with armor swaps. Preserve the shared hero skeleton and slots. The game equips hero helmets, armor, and weapons at runtime.

For an existing model replacement, preserve the required clip names, loop modes, attachment and node contracts, dimensions, GLB path and Godot UID. A stable bone name alone does not preserve the motion on a changed bind pose. Retarget and check the resulting motion.

## Export and verify in Godot

1. Keep one current editable Blender source with named meshes, packed palette images, and any needed actions. Put raw reconstructions, interim meshes, draft exports, and review captures under the OS temporary work root. Export a staged self-contained GLB with the correct scale, axis conversion, skins, materials, and clips. Check that weights, normals, UVs and the intended texture survive import.
2. Install the reviewed GLB at the intended game art path. Preserve import settings and the UID when replacing existing art. Compose or update the actor or equipment scene separately. The [monster authoring guide](../docs/monster-authoring-design.md) covers behavior, collisions and other gameplay data.
3. Spawn the enemy in the [regular combat harness](../docs/combat-harness.md) at the default distance. Judge it at the actual game-camera angle, with the default lighting and the equipped weapon. Use close views to inspect fit. Review idle, walk, each gameplay attack and the transitions, and check shoulder plates, elbows, straps, head clearance and weapon grip. Sample intermediate frames around tight clearance. For wearable art, use the actual equipped hero and validate the separate display model in inventory or item preview. Confirm the game rig, render layers and materials before publishing a final catalog image.

Register a rigged humanoid with a `blender_source` recipe, following the [Orc recipe](characters/orc/orc_warrior/character.json). The Studio opens the saved Blender file in a separate process, builds a skinned GLB, and verifies the actual Godot import on export. Re-exporting an unchanged source writes byte-identical files. The recipe records the scene objects to export, animation roles and loop modes. The Blender source owns the mesh, weights, palette images and actions. See [maintenance](maintenance/character-and-wearable-recipes.md) for earlier recipe builders.
